# Shipped RTS engines are built around command streams

Every mature RTS engine examined (0 A.D./Pyrogenesis, Spring/Recoil, OpenRA, Stratagus, Warzone 2100, plus the documented parts of Age of Empires, Supreme Commander, Planetary Annihilation, StarCraft II and Company of Heroes) has the same core: a fixed-tick simulation that only changes through commands scheduled for a future tick, with determinism tooling, serialization, spatial queries, vision, per-unit order queues and a data-definition layer around it. Assets, maps, camera and unit logic sit on top of that core. The four-item list leaves out the parts that cost most to retrofit. The command pipeline, deterministic math and RNG split, state hashing and a headless sim are needed on day one. Spatial indexing, fog of war, AI-through-commands, data-driven definitions, replays, debug metering and a crowd-rendering stress test are needed before the first playable battle. Save/load, layered AI, mission triggers, editors and the asset pipeline become blocking at alpha. A Napoleonic game adds simulation components that StarCraft-lineage engines do not have: timed formation changes, aggregated musketry calibrated to hit rates of roughly 1 in 300–800 rounds, scalar morale with contagion and rally, courier-delayed orders, fatigue/ammunition/fouling, and smoke as a visibility field. The browser adds one sourced hazard that decides the design: `Math.sin` and other transcendental functions give different results across JS engines and versions, so the 20 Hz sim should use fixed-point integer state in typed arrays. The rest (worker hosting, COOP/COEP, WebGL2 limits, Babylon tree-shaking, storage eviction) is manageable when planned for. Napoleonic games publish almost no official formulas, so much of the game-mechanic evidence below comes from previews, forums and fan readings of data files. Each claim is tagged accordingly.

**How to read the tags.** [primary] means developer-authored source code, articles, talks or official docs. [secondary] means press, fan summaries or third-party blogs. [forum] means player or modder reports. [unverified] means the research notes recalled the claim from background knowledge and did not confirm it against a fetched source. [inference] means a design judgement drawn from the sources, not a sourced fact. The ranking of subsystems by "when it bites" in section 2 is practitioner judgement from the research notes. No shipped project published it.

## 1. Five layers cover every engine examined

The open-source codebases give the most reliable subsystem map, because the module structure can be read directly from the source trees. 0 A.D.'s `source/` splits into `simulation2` (a deterministic entity-component sim with message passing), `graphics`/`renderer`, `network`, `lobby`, `gui`, `scriptinterface`, `ps` (engine core), `lib` (platform), `soundmanager`, `i18n`, `maths`, `collada` and `tools` ([0ad source tree](https://github.com/0ad/0ad/tree/master/source)) [primary]. Recoil's `rts/` splits into `Sim`, `Rendering`, `Lua`, `Net`, `Map`, `Game`, `System`, `ExternalAI`, `Menu` and an RmlUi-based UI ([RecoilEngine rts/](https://github.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/tree/master/rts)) [primary]. OpenRA keeps a game-agnostic `OpenRA.Game` core and puts nearly all gameplay in `Mods.*` assemblies driven by YAML trait bags ([OpenRA tree](https://github.com/OpenRA/OpenRA/tree/bleed)) [primary]. Warzone 2100 is flat, but it still separates a reusable `lib/` (renderer, netplay, sound, widget, map library, fixed-point math) from game code in `src/` ([wz2100 lib](https://github.com/Warzone2100/warzone2100/tree/master/lib)) [primary].

The table consolidates these into five layers. Each row has a one-line purpose and at least one named example. Purposes that come from directory and file names, rather than documentation, count as inference from self-describing code.

Two architectural patterns run across all of these. First, the native/script boundary sits at spatial and global managers, not between "engine" and "game". 0 A.D. keeps position, obstruction, pathfinding, range/LOS, motion and territory in C++, while UnitAI, Attack, Health, Formation and ProductionQueue are JavaScript ([components dir](https://github.com/0ad/0ad/tree/master/source/simulation2/components); [public mod](https://github.com/0ad/0ad/tree/master/binaries/data/mods/public/simulation/components)) [primary]. For this project that is a sourced argument for keeping grids, pathing and spatial queries in tight typed-array code (and possibly WASM later), and putting formation behaviour in plainer TypeScript [inference]. Second, two lineages exist for networking and replay. Deterministic lockstep with command replays (AoE primary; SC2 and SupCom unverified) produces tiny replays that break across patches. Authoritative-server state streaming (Planetary Annihilation's keyframed "curves" at a 10 Hz server tick) produces version-tolerant, seekable replays and does not need determinism ([ChronoCam](https://www.forrestthewoods.com/blog/tech_of_planetary_annihilation_chrono_cam/)) [primary]. The planned architecture is in the first lineage.

## 2. What the four-item list misses, ranked by when each piece bites

The naive list (assets, maps, camera, unit logic) covers the visible game. The open-source survey found that every engine also contains a command pipeline, sync hashing, replays, spatial queries, vision, order queues, a projectile/damage pipeline, a data layer with inheritance and validation, authority-tiered scripting, serialization, and in-repo tooling ([open-source notes, sources above](https://github.com/0ad/0ad/tree/master/source)). The timing column follows the research notes' legend. **Day one** means it must exist before the first unit moves. **Prototype** means before the first playable battle. **Alpha** means before content production scales. **Ship** means production polish. These rankings are practitioner judgement [inference] supported by the cited evidence.

|---|---|---|
| **Day one** | Command format and tick scheduling | Everything hangs off the command stream: replays, saves-as-logs, AI, tests, future lockstep. AoE sent only commands, because per-unit state would have capped the game at about 250 moving units ([1500 Archers](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/1500-archers-on-a-28-8-network-programming-in-age-of-empires-and-beyond)) [primary]. Single-player can use 0–1 tick input delay, but commands still need a tick stamp and a serialisable form [inference]. |
| **Day one** | Deterministic math and RNG split | JS `Math.sin` differs between engines and even V8 versions ([bounce issue #8](https://codeberg.org/perplexdotgg/bounce/issues/8)) [primary]. AoE had to make "the same number of random calls on every machine" [primary]. Retrofitting fixed-point into a float sim touches every system [inference]. |

| **Alpha** | Notifications, minimap, localization, colour-blind team ID | Cheap if planned, expensive to retrofit [inference; no primary source fetched]. |
| **Ship** | Hotkey rebinding, audio mix (ducking, intensity beds), accessibility polish, crash telemetry, offline/PWA | Diablo IV's mix uses voice limiting, importance and ducking together [primary listing]; Bannerlord builds battle ambience from individual sounds ([TaleWorlds blog](http://www.taleworlds.com/en/Games/Bannerlord/Blog/95)) [primary; details not retrieved]. |

Two items deserve a note for this project specifically. The research found **no primary source for cross-browser floating-point determinism in JS beyond the single physics-engine bug report**, and the notes flag it as the largest unverified risk. That moves determinism testing across Chrome, Firefox and Safari into the prototype stage rather than leaving it until lockstep [inference]. Second, AoE measured an average of one command every 1.5–2 s, spiking to 3–4 per second in battles, and a repeat-click filter "greatly reduced" spikes ([1500 Archers](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/1500-archers-on-a-28-8-network-programming-in-age-of-empires-and-beyond)) [primary]. Orders aimed at 20–40 formation entities rather than individual figures keep the command stream in that range [inference].

## 3. Napoleonic battles need drill, morale and delay as engine systems

None of the Napoleonic-specific mechanics below have official published formulas. The historical parameters come from secondary summaries of Nosworthy, Nafziger, Bremm, Haythornthwaite and others, not from the books. The game mechanics come mostly from previews, store pages, fan readings of data files and forum posts.

### Formations are wide, shallow, and vulnerable while changing

A French battalion after the 1808 reorganisation had **6 companies of about 100 men (33 files × 3 ranks each)**. In line that made a front of **about 111 m and 2.3 m deep**, stretching to about 139 m in battle, while a 2-rank 600-man battalion was about 168 m wide ([Balagan](https://balagan.info/battalion-line-column-and-square-tactical-formations-during-the-napoleonic-wars)) [secondary]. The official frontage per man was 22 in (56 cm), sometimes 27–30 in in battle, with Nafziger giving 26 in for the French. The **attack column was about 40 men wide and 18 deep**, and company spacing in column was 10 yd (half-distance), 5 yd (quarter-distance) or a full company frontage of about 20 yd (open column). Mutually supporting squares stood about 90 m apart (same source) [secondary]. Formation-change times are the most important numbers for gameplay: **column to square about 60 s (Nafziger), line to square within 100 s by regulation, forming attack column about 30 s, and charging cavalry closing to contact in about 60 s** (same source) [secondary]. March rates: British ordinary pace was 30 in at 80 steps/min, quick pace 120/min (about 61 and 91 m/min), and the French 1791 regulation was about 2 French feet at 76/min ordinary or 100/min accelerated ([Founders Online](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Hamilton/01-24-02-0343)) [primary letter, read via search snippet]. Skirmishers made up 1 of 10 companies in a British battalion and 1 of 6 in a French one, with Prussian, Austrian and Russian battalions using about double that ([N. F. Taylor](https://www.oocities.org/nftaylorscotland/towtbtf.html)) [secondary].

Games represent this in three ways. **Empire: Total War models every man and every musket ball individually**, so a man behind a tree is better protected than one in the open ([bit-tech preview](https://www.bit-tech.net/previews/gaming/pc/empire-total-war-hands-on-preview/4/)) [secondary, pre-release]. **Ultimate General: Gettysburg treats a brigade of hundreds as one deformable line** ([DSOGaming](https://www.dsogaming.com/?p=52452)) [secondary]. A player found that one brigade turning to face skirmishers dropped the whole brigade's morale and opened a gap, which shows the cost of the single-unit abstraction ([TWCenter](https://www.twcenter.net/threads/first-impressions-ultimate-general-gettysburg.659538/)) [forum]. **Scourge of War uses officer-led formation templates**: a brigadier given only a move order may leave his troops behind, while a formation order makes all of them move and deploy ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/369390/discussions/0/485622866449190006)) [forum].

Applied to this project's numbers [inference]: a formation of 100–200 figures standing for a 500–800-man battalion implies a figure-to-man ratio of roughly 1:3 to 1:8. A 1–1.5 km play area fits only about 8–12 battalion lines abreast, so 20–40 formations means brigade- to division-scale battles in two or more lines. At 61–91 m/min, crossing 1 km takes 11–16 minutes of game time, and one figure moves about 0.05–0.08 m per 20 Hz tick. Q16.16 fixed-point in an `Int32Array` gives 1/65,536 m resolution and a ±32 km range, which covers this easily. The 60 s cavalry approach against 60–100 s to form square equals 1,200 ticks against 1,200–2,000 ticks. A battalion therefore needs a formation entity with width, depth, rank count, facing and file spacing, and **formation change should be a timed transition state during which the unit is penalised**. The 120 m × 2 m line shape argues for treating a line as a deformable polyline over terrain, as UG appears to, rather than a rigid rectangle. No developer document on UG's line deformation was found.

### Musketry must be calibrated to battlefield, not range, hit rates

The **Prussian 1810 test against a 90 m × 1.8 m target gave 60% hits at 70 m, 40% at 140 m and 5% at 210 m**, with up to 20% misfires in good conditions (Bremm) ([Military History Visualized](https://militaryhistoryvisualized.com/tag/muskets)) [secondary]. Battlefield estimates are 100–300× lower: **Napier about 1 hit per 300 shots, Guibert about 1 in 500, Vitoria about 800 rounds per casualty** (Nosworthy) (same source), and Barbero gives 1 in 459 overall and 1 in 162 at Waterloo ([MHV snippet](https://militaryhistoryvisualized.com/tag/rate-of-fire/)) [secondary, via snippet]. Duhèsme's battalion caused only 3–4 casualties in a long firefight at 100 paces (about 76 m) (Nosworthy). **Practical rate of fire was about 2–3 rounds per minute** against a theoretical 4–5, and barrels became too hot after about 25 rounds or 8 minutes of continuous fire. **Smoke cut visibility of large formations to about 45 m** (60 paces), with one journalist claiming 20 paces (all from [MHV](https://militaryhistoryvisualized.com/tag/muskets)) [secondary]. An 1813 Prussian trial at 100 paces scored 92 (Old Prussian), 94 (British) and 151 (French musket) hits out of 200 ([N. F. Taylor](https://www.oocities.org/nftaylorscotland/towtbtf.html)) [secondary]. Taylor says Weller's "regular 15-second volleys" rest on "so little evidence".

The game models split here. Napoleon: Total War mods tune scatter, effective range, reload, muzzle velocity and ammunition per weapon ([ModDB](https://www.moddb.com/mods/rebs-ballistic-realism-north-south-submod)) [forum/mod]. They also use a weapon "reliability" value so that with a 33% misfire rate only part of the front rank fires in the first volley ([IMTW](https://imtw.org/topic/6778-napoleonic-napoleon-empire-realism/)) [forum/mod]. Ultimate General shows a "reload gauge" as the percentage of men loaded, which makes reload an aggregate brigade state ([tvtropes summary](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/UltimateGeneralGettysburg)) [secondary]. UG: Civil War weapons carry range and accuracy stats scaled by brigade "efficiency" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/502520/discussions/0/1620599015900872857)) [forum].

The design consequence is that a naive 40% hit rate at 140 m would wipe out a battalion in one or two volleys, so per-shot probabilities need stress, smoke and fatigue multipliers that bring them down to the 1-in-300-to-800 range [inference]. For calibration: 600 men at 2–3 rounds/min fire 1,200–1,800 rounds/min, which at 1 hit per 300–500 rounds gives roughly **3–6 casualties per minute**, consistent with Duhèsme's 3–4 [inference]. That suggests aggregated per-formation musketry (fraction loaded, volleys resolved statistically, musket balls as render-only particles) rather than per-ball physics at this figure count [inference]. Per-formation misfire or desynchronised reload avoids perfectly synchronised volleys [inference, following the NTW mods].

### Artillery is the one place ballistic simulation pays

Sources on artillery conflict. French 8-pounder and British 9-pounder roundshot was effective to **about 800 yd, bouncing ("second graze") a considerable distance after first impact**, and **the British normally did not use canister beyond 350 yd** ([HistoryOfWar](https://www.historyofwar.org/articles/weapons_artillery_napoleonic.html)) [secondary]. A retailer page gives 6-pounder roundshot effective at 800–900 yd and maximum about 1,700 yd ([Lanes Armoury](https://thelanesarmoury.co.uk/shop.php?code=25987)) [low reliability]. A forum estimate puts canister usually at about 200 m and notes that the ground between gun and target governed ricochet ([TWCenter](https://www.twcenter.net/threads/napoleonic-gunnery.409042/)) [forum]. A wargaming figure of 55 hits at 200 yd falling to 6 at 600 yd has no stated conditions ([Warlord Games](https://www.warlordgames.com/?p=84074)) [flagged uncertain]. Empire: Total War models every cannonball with physics so that shots bounce along the ground through lines ([GameSpy preview via TWCenter](https://www.twcenter.net/threads/gamespy-preview.186702/)) [secondary]. Because roundshot effect depends on the ground, roundshot fits a deterministic ray bouncing along the heightfield, and canister fits a cone [inference]. With a few dozen guns firing about once per several seconds, the cost is small next to musketry [inference]. Limber/unlimber times, howitzer fuzes and friendly fire had no sources (the note recalls about 1 minute for unlimbering [unverified]).

### Melee and cavalry turn on the formation-change window

Documented game mechanics for melee were not found. The historical pattern is that steady squares usually beat cavalry, but squares caught forming or broken by chance were destroyed. At **Albuera (1811) cavalry destroyed three of Colborne's regiments within minutes**, at Wagram (1809) the 20th Chasseurs à Cheval broke an Austrian square, and at García Hernández (1812) King's German Legion dragoons broke a steady French square ([Balagan](https://balagan.info/battalion-line-column-and-square-tactical-formations-during-the-napoleonic-wars)) [secondary]. UG lets flank attacks rout even strong units ([DSOGaming](https://www.dsogaming.com/?p=52452)) [secondary]. A charge resolved as velocity/mass impact plus a morale shock test, with a large square multiplier against cavalry, a penalty for units in transition, and a small breach probability on a morale failure, reproduces these cases [inference]. There are no sourced charge-momentum formulas (Total War charge bonus or mass), bayonet-charge resolution or pursuit rules. The historians' claim that most bayonet charges ended before contact was not verified.

### Morale is a scalar with event shocks, contagion and a general's radius

Every game reviewed uses a scalar morale shifted by casualties, flank or rear attack, nearby routs, artillery, fatigue and generals, with threshold states driving behaviour. **Fan-read values from older Total War files are: flanks secure +4, flanks exposed −1, exposed on multiple sides −2, attacked in flank −3, attacked in rear −5, with routing-ally contagion out to 30 units to the flank and 75 to the front** ([TWCenter: On morale](https://www.twcenter.net/threads/on-morale.404713/)) [forum; may have changed with patches, not Napoleon-specific]. The Rome II manual documents a yellow banner for a morale penalty and red for wavering, a rally ability that works only on routers inside the general's radius, and units becoming **"shattered" and unable to rally if the general dies while they rout** ([Rome II manual](https://r2enc.totalwar.com/en/manual/single-player/0087_enc_page_battle_play_phase_conflict_morale)) [primary]. Single factors rarely break a healthy unit unless they stack with heavy damage ([ACOUP](https://acoup.blog/2022/07/01/collections-total-generalship-commanding-pre-modern-armies-part-iiic-morale-and-cohesion/comment-page-1/)) [secondary]. UG lists flank and rear attacks, casualties, volley shock, artillery and fatigue as morale inputs, and makes demoralised troops unresponsive ([DSOGaming](https://www.dsogaming.com/?p=52452)) [secondary]. The engine component is a per-formation morale scalar with additive per-tick modifiers and event shocks feeding a state machine (steady → shaken → wavering → routing → rallied or shattered), with the general's aura as a modifier and rally gate [inference]. That is a direct consumer of the modifier stack in section 2.

### Command delay is a simulation system, not a UI effect

The command-focused games simulate order latency. **Grand Tactician computes delay from communication method, distance, commander Initiative, order type and number of recipients**. Orders cascade Army HQ → Corps → Division → brigades, each HQ prepares one order at a time, bugle signals are faster, couriers cannot be intercepted, and in-transit orders appear as animated arrows ([MagicGameWorld](https://www.magicgameworld.com/?p=55099)) [secondary]. **Scourge of War sends one courier per order to the brigadier** and lets the player enable couriers only above a chosen command level ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/369390/discussions/0/485622866449190006)) [forum, developer reply]. Incoming reports can be 30 minutes stale ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/369390/discussions/0/1740013516079255375)) [forum]. Command Ops 2 lists order delay as one of four design pillars ([Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/521800/Command_Ops_2/)) [primary marketing]. UG has no courier delay; it delegates through autonomous behaviour and seven AI personalities ([DSOGaming](https://www.dsogaming.com/?p=52452)) [secondary]. In tabletop rules, Black Powder resolves one command roll per brigade ([Warlord forum](https://www.warlordgames.com/community/topic/question-about-orders/)) [forum], and General d'Armée uses an ADC system with a C-in-C over 5–8 brigades ([1st Corps](https://1stcorps.co.uk/product/general-darmee-rule-book)) [secondary].

This fits the planned "commands are the only input" architecture well [inference]. A player command becomes a sim entity (a courier or an order in preparation) that travels the command graph and changes the recipient's orders only on arrival, so delay stays deterministic and replayable. Recipients keep executing their last order until then. SoW's "couriers above level X" setting works as a difficulty dial. The staff-map view is the natural place to show orders in transit and stale reports. No numeric courier speeds or preparation times were found for any game.

### Fatigue, ammunition, terrain and smoke are separate accumulators and fields

UG:G folds ammunition and fatigue into one "Condition" stat that recovers while standing still out of combat ([tvtropes summary](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/UltimateGeneralGettysburg)) [secondary]. Historically, fouling and heat capped sustained fire after about 25 rounds ([MHV](https://militaryhistoryvisualized.com/tag/muskets)) [secondary], and NTW realism mods give rifles +5% fouling ([IMTW](https://imtw.org/topic/6778-napoleonic-napoleon-empire-realism/)) [forum/mod]. Keeping fatigue, rounds remaining and fouling as separate sim values, and combining them only in the UI, keeps both realism and readability [inference]. The note recalls about 50–60 cartridges per man [unverified]. In UG, high ground gives longer range, better accuracy and a morale bonus ([DSOGaming](https://www.dsogaming.com/?p=52452)) [secondary]. Empire: Total War has garrisonable buildings and destructible walls ([bit-tech](https://www.bit-tech.net/previews/gaming/pc/empire-total-war-hands-on-preview/4/)) [secondary]. In SoW, road movement orders speed travel and enemies are visible only within 1,000 yd on Normal ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/369390/discussions/0/1740013516079255375)) [forum]. The terrain system therefore needs a heightfield used for LOS, elevation bonuses, slope cost and roundshot bounce, plus cover polygons with garrison slots, a road network with a speed multiplier, linear water with fords and bridges that force column, and a **dynamic smoke field** that feeds both line of sight (down toward 45 m) and fire accuracy [inference]. No numeric slope, woods-cover or ford rules were found in any game source.

## 4. The browser forces fixed-point math and a worker-hosted sim

The browser concerns that change architecture rather than tuning are determinism, threading, memory, rendering limits, delivery and tab lifecycle. With an RTX 3060 laptop as min spec, the iOS and Android limits below matter mainly if mobile is ever targeted.

**Determinism.** ECMAScript leaves the precision of `Math.sin` and its relatives to implementations, and fdlibm is "recommended but not required" ([esdiscuss](https://esdiscuss.org/topic/es6-accuracy-of-special-functions)) [primary]. In a 2026 bug report on a JS physics engine, **Node and Chrome (both V8, different versions) produced different `Math.sin` results while `Math.sqrt` matched**. The divergence compounded every step until the author ported fdlibm kernels using only `+ − × /` ([bounce issue #8](https://codeberg.org/perplexdotgg/bounce/issues/8)) [primary]. V8 moved sin/cos from fdlibm to a glibc-derived implementation ([Mozilla bug 1823880](https://bug623317.bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1823880)) [primary], and a report that Chrome 148's `Math.tanh` calls the platform `std::tanh` is [unverified] ([GeekNews](https://hi.news.hada.io/topic?id=31380)). Basic IEEE 754 operations are exactly specified, so a float sim can be deterministic if it avoids transcendentals. Fixed-point removes the question entirely, including compiler FMA contraction if WASM is used later [inference]. In WASM, NaN bit patterns are nondeterministic and relaxed SIMD trades determinism for speed ([WebAssembly nondeterminism](https://www.webassembly.org/docs/nondeterminism/); [Wasmtime](https://docs.wasmtime.dev/examples-deterministic-wasm-execution.html)) [primary]. The project's typed-array plan fits fixed-point: Q16.16 in `Int32Array`, lookup-table or polynomial trig, a seeded PRNG, no `Math.random`, and entity-ID-ordered iteration [inference]. JS `Map`/`Set` iterate in insertion order, which is deterministic only if insertion order is [inference].

**Threading and hosting.** A sim in one dedicated worker that posts a per-tick snapshot as a transferable `ArrayBuffer` needs no special headers [inference from MDN transferables, canonical ref not fetched]. SharedArrayBuffer and WASM threads require cross-origin isolation (COOP `same-origin` + COEP `require-corp` or `credentialless`) ([web.dev](https://web.dev/cross-origin-isolation-guide)) [primary]. Isolation then blocks any cross-origin resource that does not opt in ([cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/tools/cross-origin-isolation-checker)) [secondary]. **Safari still lacks COEP `credentialless`** ([WebKit bug 230550](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=230550)) [primary; no status change confirmed after Jan 2025]. **GitHub Pages cannot set the headers** ([GitHub discussion #13309](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/13309)) [primary]; Cloudflare Pages and Netlify can via `_headers` [secondary]. A single-threaded deterministic sim gains little from SAB at 20 Hz, so transferables are the low-friction default and SAB is an upgrade only for multithreaded WASM pathfinding [inference]. Running the tick loop in the worker also matters for lifecycle: rAF stops in hidden tabs, and Chrome's intensive throttling limits main-thread timer wake-ups to one per minute for hidden pages after a grace period ([blink-dev intent](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/5SZB2CFFGqE/m/-mtBskjyAgAJ)) [primary]. Single-player should simply pause on `visibilitychange` [inference]. Whether worker timers escape intensive throttling in 2026 is [unverified].

**Rendering on WebGL2 with Babylon 9.** WebGPU ships by default in Chrome/Edge, Safari 26 and Firefox on Windows and Apple Silicon macOS, while Linux and Android Firefox lag ([gpuweb status](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/wiki/implementation-status)) [primary], so WebGL2 remains the baseline [inference]. Babylon 9.0 (March 2026) runs clustered lighting on both backends ([Windows Mode summary](https://www.windowsmode.com/babylon-js-9-0-released-build-immersive-3d-web-apps/)) [secondary; official post not fetched]. **Thin instances are one mesh plus a matrix buffer with no per-instance JS objects, but culling is all-or-nothing over a bounding box covering every instance**, and mixing positive and negative determinant matrices renders incorrectly ([Babylon docs](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/mesh/copies/thinInstances)) [primary]. For 2k–8k figures that suggests one thin-instanced mesh per figure type per LOD per spatial chunk, with per-instance attributes for team colour and VAT animation frame, written each render frame from interpolated snapshots [inference]. Babylon VAT support has been discussed on the forum ([forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/vertex-animation-textures/6325)) [forum]. The staff-map view at far zoom is where the far LOD ends: SupCom's strategic zoom swapping units for icons is the recalled precedent [unverified], and with a 2D map at far zoom the 3D crowd never has to render at full-battlefield scale [inference]. Texture budget: **WebGL2 `MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE` 8192 is available on 94.5% of devices and 16384 on 95.8% of Windows devices** but about 2% of Android ([Web3DSurvey](https://web3dsurvey.com/webgl2/parameters/MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE)) [primary data]. GPU timer queries reach about 72% of Chromium sessions and almost no Safari ([Web3DSurvey](https://web3dsurvey.com/webgl2/extensions/EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2)) [primary], so they are a dev tool, not field telemetry. Babylon context-loss recovery is unreliable in practice and several developers force a reload ([Babylon forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/trying-to-get-a-handle-on-webgl-context-lost/19848)) [forum]. A sim kept in a worker survives context loss untouched, so rebuilding the scene from sim state, with `doNotHandleContextLost: true` to save memory, is the simpler path [inference].

**Delivery and build.** Babylon reads `KHR_texture_basisu` (KTX2), Draco, `EXT_meshopt_compression` (5.0+, no setup) and `KHR_mesh_quantization` ([Babylon KTX2 docs](https://doc.babylonjs.com/features/featuresDeepDive/materials/using/ktx2Compression)) [primary]. Meshopt decodes faster than Draco with a decoder about 10× smaller and also compresses animation ([cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/optimize-glb-for-web)) [secondary]. ETC1S suits albedo and UASTC suits normals/ORM (Babylon docs) [primary]. Importing from the `@babylonjs/core` root pulls in the whole library because modules have side effects. Deep imports fix that, and the `@babylonjs/core/pure` barrel shipped in 9.8.0 (May 2026) tree-shakes with explicit `Register*()` calls, though the team says it is "NOT a magical solution" ([Babylon forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/pure-barrel-for-better-tree-shaking-in-babylonjs-core/63455)) [primary]. Missing side-effect imports fail at runtime, not build time ([forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/whats-the-recommended-way-to-do-es6-typescript-imports/16963)) [forum]. Keeping the sim package free of Babylon imports lets the same replay run in Node for determinism CI and in each browser for cross-engine checks [inference].

**Storage, audio and input.** Cache Storage, IndexedDB and OPFS share one per-origin quota. **Safari deletes script-written storage after 7 days without interaction**, eviction is whole-origin, and `navigator.storage.persist()` exempts an origin from LRU eviction ([MDN storage quotas](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria)) [primary]. Saves as seed + map + command log + periodic snapshot are small, but should also be exportable as files [inference]. Babylon's audio engine v2 resolves `CreateAudioEngineAsync()` without a gesture, but `unlockAsync()` stays pending until one, and awaiting it at boot stalls rendering ([Babylon forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/best-way-to-init-audio-engine-v2-without-interrupting-code-flow/59321)) [forum, core team]. Whether AudioV2 has built-in voice priority is [unverified], so a per-formation emitter and global voice budget is likely custom work [inference]. Hotkeys should bind by `KeyboardEvent.code` and display by `key` ([Chrome dev blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/keyboardevent-keys-codes/)) [primary]. `unadjustedMovement` pointer lock was Chromium-only as of the cited 2020 article ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/disable-mouse-acceleration)) [primary, dated]. RTS-specific input issues follow from these: right-click `contextmenu` suppression, edge-pan failing when the cursor leaves a windowed canvas, browser-reserved shortcuts (Ctrl+W/T/N) that cannot be captured outside Chromium Keyboard Lock in fullscreen, and capping effective DPR [inference].

## Conclusion

The sources support one design rule above the others. The Napoleonic systems (timed formation changes, courier delay, morale contagion, smoke) and the browser constraints (fixed-point math, a worker-hosted sim, context-loss recovery) both fit the deterministic command-driven core described in section 1. Courier delay becomes a sim entity in flight, the staff map becomes a view over the same sim and fog state, a lost WebGL context becomes a renderer rebuild from untouched worker state, and debug cheats become commands that stay replayable. Each of these is cheap only if the command pipeline, fixed-point state, RNG split and hashing exist before the first formation moves. The single largest open risk is cross-browser numeric divergence. Evidence for it is one V8-only bug report plus the spec's latitude, so a Chrome/Firefox/Safari replay-hash comparison belongs in the first prototype.

The Napoleonic evidence base is thin where it matters most for tuning. No game publishes its morale, charge or fire formulas, the Total War morale numbers are fan-read from older titles, and courier speeds, slope penalties and limbering times are unsourced. Historical anchors such as the 60–100 s square-forming window and the 1-in-300-to-800 battlefield hit rate are well enough attested to calibrate against. Exact game-feel parameters will have to come from the project's own playtesting, using the metering and replay tooling the RTS lineage treats as permanent infrastructure.
