# Clamp formed bodies, suspend queues, never shove

Treat formation collision as a hard non-penetration constraint between a few thousand bin rectangles, not as a force between men. Nothing should push anything: a formed body that meets another formed body stops at the contact line, and only the bins that touch stop. Their neighbours keep moving up to a shear limit, so the line bends around the point of contact. Ground is given only as the result of combat, never as the result of collision. Whether two bodies may overlap depends on their state, not their mass. Formed bodies never overlap, while routers, skirmishers, a battalion in a passage of lines and fleeing gunners are flagged "loose" and pass through friends at a disorder and speed cost. A charge that breaks a battalion does not punch through it. It turns the battalion loose, and only then can the cavalry ride over the ground. The evidence for this comes mostly from failures. Total War, Cossacks and Bannerlord collide individual soldiers with mass, and players report the same two failure modes in all of them: melee "blobs", and fast or heavy units running straight through formed lines. The open-source engines (0 A.D., Recoil, OpenRA) show how to make contact resolution deterministic and order-independent: read one snapshot, visit pairs in canonical ID order, accumulate, then apply. The group-formation literature shows that colliding the group as one entity, while members deviate locally, keeps formations intact through obstacles at a few percent overhead. At 16,000–32,000 men this sim has about 240–480 company blocks and perhaps 1,000–4,000 bins, which is cheap to collide exactly. Commands should follow the convention every shipped game shares. A plain order replaces the queue, Shift appends, and Stop or Halt clears. The current rule that a charge or melee clears the queue should go. No sourced game clears a queue on combat contact. Interruptions should instead be classed as part of the order (resume at once), a forced reaction under command (suspend, then resume once the head order is re-checked) or loss of command (clear). No shipped game documents resume-after-interruption behaviour, so that part is design, not precedent.

**How to read this report.** Claims with a link are sourced. Where a claim is my design judgement and not a fact from a source, the text says so ("I recommend", "inference", "design"). Where the notes in this folder disagree with the earlier report *Formation movement and doc systems*, the notes here win. One example is numerics: the earlier report assumed Q16.16 fixed point, and this brief specifies restricted float64. This report stands on its own.

## Per-soldier mass collision gives either a blob or a breakthrough

Total War's Warscape engine makes every soldier a collision body. The `battle_entities` table gives each entity a "radius" that "determines the distance at which the unit model will collide with other models around it". A shield-wall tutorial drops it to 0.4 so that packed models stop colliding, which shows that formation density and collision radius are tuned against each other ([TW Modding wiki](https://tw-modding.com/wiki/Creating_a_Shield_Wall_formation)). The formation itself is not a rigid shape. It is a spacing table (`unit_spacings`, with close and loose horizontal and vertical spacing, "spacing variation" and a "Routing Space Variation" column) that the entities steer toward (same source). Bracing works through mass. Pharaoh's 1.1.0 patch fixed a case where "Charge Reflect wouldn't appropriately increase the mass of a unit when bracing", and the same notes cut chariot mass by about 10% and reduced chariot collision size. That detail comes from a search summary of the [official patch notes](https://www.totalwar.com/news/total-war-pharaoh-patch-notes-110); the page itself returned 429. Modders show that physical collision and melee engagement use separate radii ("model collision radius & combat reaction radius") ([Workshop mod 2854020051](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2854020051)). They also show that chariot radius can be widened "to allow for easier line breakthroughs" ([Workshop mod 3008997550](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/3008997550?p=2)).

Players report two outcomes of this model, and they pull in opposite directions. The first is the blob. One player wrote that "matched combat was introduced with the warscape engine and it's awful. Units combat animating into each other broke up unit cohesion" ([WH2 discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/594570/discussions/0/3076496088114787038)). In Rome II and Attila, melee "becomes one large crowd" ([Attila discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/325610/discussions/0/2592234299550263723)). The second is pass-through. Players in Rome II and Pharaoh report units walking through formed lines, sometimes without losses ([Rome II workshop discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/workshop/filedetails/discussion/294694062/3570700856124390658)). Pharaoh players have even asked for older behaviour to be restored ([Pharaoh discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1937780/discussions/0/6929409304786113872)). Making formations stiffer has its own cost. Pharaoh players say the spear-wall stance makes units fight "significantly worse", because fewer men engage ([Pharaoh discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1937780/discussions/0/3820788154600871737/)). Creative Assembly keeps retuning mass and collision size as a single knob. My inference is that this is structural: in a mass-based model, more mass means breakthroughs and less mass means stalls into an interpenetrated crowd, and no setting of the knob avoids both.

The other per-soldier games repeat the pattern. Cossacks 3 runs formations of **36 to 400** men that, according to players, lose formation as soon as they walk or attack. Melee units "break into an unruly mob", and units scatter when friends walk through them ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/333420/discussions/0/350542683210253274)). Bannerlord's developers describe a formation AI that issues orders but "doesn't directly make them do anything: this is left to the individual AI" ([TaleWorlds blog via GameBanshee](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k4h4h)). Players report units that "push their way through enemies if you concentrate enough bodies in a single point" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/261550/discussions/0/3198118348349793435)). In Scourge of War: Waterloo, units halt "partially or even mostly behind friendly units" and fire, which suggests friends barely separate at all ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/369390/discussions/0/1696043263498441739)).

Game-Labs' Ultimate General is closest to this project in abstraction: one unit is one shape, casualties are counts, and units "bunched too closely will spread out enough to be able to fire unhindered" ([TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/UltimateGeneralGettysburg)). The automatic separation had a cost. Patch 1.7 promised "Improved auto AI lines. No longer too much shifting around" and stopped infantry from auto-falling back and "annoyingly" losing position ([RPG Codex quoting patch notes](https://rpgcodex.net/forums/threads/ultimate-general-gettysburg.95450/)). Its melee is a quick, lethal contact state, and a reviewer called it too frequent and too bloody for the period ([Wargamer](https://www.wargamer.com/reviews/review-ultimate-general-civil-war/)). Total War: Arena chose a softer rule. Slingers that overlapped stopped firing, and the fix made overlapping fire cause minor friendly fire, so overlap carries a cost instead of being forbidden ([MP1st](https://mp1st.com/news/latest-total-war-arena-update-fixes-blobbing-slinger-issue)).

None of the surveyed games has a documented, well-regarded contact model, and none of the studios has published how theirs works. What players praise is cohesion: older Total War games where a line held even as models fell, and Grand Tactician's leapfrogging and passage of lines in a fighting withdrawal ([GT Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/654890/discussions/0/787664008885842907)). History points the same way. The earlier research found Larrey counting **119 musket wounds to 5 bayonet wounds**, and columns at La Rothière halting at 30 paces until one side broke ([Napoleonistyka 2](https://www.napolun.com/mirror/napoleonistyka.atspace.com/infantry_tactics_2.htm)). Du Picq claimed, exaggerating, that one side fled first in about 49 of 50 cavalry charges ([Napoleonistyka cavalry](https://www.napolun.com/mirror/napoleonistyka.atspace.com/cavalry_tactics.html)). Contact between formed bodies was rare and short, and the usual outcome was that one side gave way. A model in which formed bodies block each other and combat decides who yields is more faithful to the period than one in which mass decides who goes through.

## Open engines make contact order-independent with snapshots and canonical pairs

0 A.D. publishes the most complete deterministic pushing model available, and its structure carries over directly. All moving units first take a tentative step. Every pair within a **20 m** bucket and its four edge-neighbour buckets is then visited once, under `a.id < b.id`. Each visit only accumulates (`a.push += …; b.push -= …`), and a separate pass applies the sums ([CCmpUnitMotion_System.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/0ad/0ad/master/source/simulation2/components/CCmpUnitMotion_System.cpp)). The push compares the average of each unit's start and tentative end positions. Arrival is checked at turn start "while units are not being moved (otherwise they won't be commutative)". Exact overlaps are broken by entity-ID parity, not by an RNG ([CCmpUnitMotion.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/0ad/0ad/master/source/simulation2/components/CCmpUnitMotion.h)). Four of its rules matter here. **Priority is a weight ratio with a cap**, the cap being there "for numerical stability … between a lightweight and a heavy unit". **Moving and idle units are separated**: "moving entities will push moving entities, but not stopped ones, and vice-versa." **Formation members form a control group** that "never push farther than themselves" and ignore each other's obstructions. And **units can be flagged `ignore`**, meaning they "won't push nor be pushed". A per-unit "pressure" value (uint8, decay 0.6 per turn) damps pushes and slows crowded units, with a speed floor of 1.5 so the crowd does not "behave a lot like viscous fluid" (same sources). Jams are handled by a failure counter, not solved. At 3 failed turns it switches pathfinder, at 10 it backs the unit up near corners, it widens the short-path search from 12 to 56 navcells, and at **35** it gives up with `MoveFailed`. The comment says this "needs to be somewhat high or moving groups of units will lead to stuck units" ([CCmpUnitMotion.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/0ad/0ad/master/source/simulation2/components/CCmpUnitMotion.h)).

Recoil, the Beyond All Reason engine, comes at the same problem from physics. Each unit tests its neighbours with a circle test, or with a separating-axis (SAT) oriented-box test when the footprint is stretched. It splits the separation by a momentum-like weight `s = m·v·c`, writes only into its own force accumulators, and applies them later. "Try to apply all collision forces, but if that will collide with static parts of the map, then only apply forces from static objects/terrain" ([GroundMoveType.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/master/rts/Sim/MoveTypes/GroundMoveType.cpp)). Recoil also shows where an "immovable" class goes wrong. Push-resistant units clogged factories, and large ships "get stuck on each other" ([Spring mantis 6365](https://springrts.com/mantis/view.php?id=6365)). OpenRA is grid occupancy with no forces at all. A blocked mover waits `WaitAverage` ticks, nudges an idle friend aside, re-paths while ignoring progressively fewer blockers (All → Stationary → Immovable → None), and finally sidesteps or gives up ([Move.cs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenRA/OpenRA/bleed/OpenRA.Mods.Common/Activities/Move/Move.cs)). It is reproducible, but the first actor to claim a cell wins, so the outcome depends on actor order. That breaks this project's order-independence rule.

Supreme Commander supplies the most important negative lesson. In SupCom 1, "rebuilding a path every time there is a collision turns into a compounding problem … causing the game to grind to a halt", so units stopped and waited instead ([Emerson, Game AI Pro ch. 23](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter23_Crowd_Pathfinding_and_Steering_Using_Flow_Field_Tiles.pdf)). Age of Empires II DE disabled "Collide & Correct … to prevent units from accidentally scattering" ([AoE2 DE update notes via Liquipedia](https://liquipedia.net/ageofempires/Age_of_Empires_II/Definitive_Edition/Dynasties_of_India/Update_82587)), which argues against large corrective impulses on formation members. The original Age of Empires found pathing alone took about **30%** of turn time, and "a small error in one unit's path could diverge the whole game" ([Bettner & Terrano](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/1500-archers-on-a-28-8-network-programming-in-age-of-empires-and-beyond)). There is no usable primary material on StarCraft II, Age of Empires IV or Company of Heroes collision, because their GDC talks are members-only.

Across the engines, priority comes from the same four sources: statics always win, a moving/idle split, an immovable class, and a capped weight ratio. Deadlocks are broken by escalating timeouts (wait, sidestep, re-path with fewer blockers, give up), never solved directly. Every one of these engines collides individuals and lets a formation controller re-slot them. None collides a rigid multi-body formation as a unit against another formation, so the block-versus-block design below is an extrapolation.

## The literature collides the group, lets members lag and gives up on per-man solvers

Per-agent crowd methods cost too much at this scale and still allow overlap. ORCA takes **8 ms for 5,000 agents on eight cores**. Its authors note that clamping velocities afterwards for kinematics means it "would not strictly guarantee avoiding collisions anymore" ([ORCA paper](https://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ORCA/publications/ORCA.pdf)). Narain et al.'s aggregate model caps density on a coarse grid and still leaves **0.12%** of agents slightly closer than the minimum distance. It also takes **34 ms for 10,000 agents** on a 3.2 GHz i7 ([Narain et al. 2009](https://ics-websites.science.uu.nl/docs/vakken/mcrws/papers_new/Narain%20et%20al%20-%202009%20-%20Aggregate%20dynamics%20for%20dense%20crowd%20simulation.pdf)). Position-based crowds reach **13.6–14.1 ms for 10,032 agents**, but only on a CUDA GPU ([Weiss et al., C&G 2019](https://web.cs.ucla.edu/~dt/papers/cag19/cag19.pdf)). Continuum Crowds admits that "two people in the same grid cell will sometimes intersect", and adds a push-apart that "does not strictly ensure that minimum distances are preserved" ([Treuille et al. 2006](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/crowd-flows/continuum-crowds.pdf)). None of these methods guarantees zero overlap. All of them assume the man is the body, and in this sim he is not.

The group-formation work fits much better. Gu and Deng treat each group "as a single entity" for inter-group avoidance and let members follow slots with local avoidance. Instead of "easily scattering agents caused by local collision avoidance, the inter-group dynamics tend to guide all the agents to avoid an obstacle as a whole". The formation layer adds **3.7–4.1%** overhead. A squad that follows a leader under an angular rotation limit "appears certain 'lag'", which is the same idea as this project's bins. Members pushed off their slot oscillate unless they stop chasing it inside a threshold of 1/20 of the formation radius ([Gu & Deng, GI 2011](https://graphics.cs.uh.edu/wp-content/uploads/papers/2011/GI2011_formationSketching_preprint.pdf)). Henry, Shum and Komura model the crowd as an as-rigid-as-possible mesh in which some regions can be kept rigid. When agents cannot keep up, the mesh slows to "allow the agents to catch up", so the shape waits for its members instead of stretching without limit ([Henry et al., TVCG 2014](https://www.hubertshum.com/publications/tvcg2014formation/files/tvcg2014formation.pdf)). Neither paper models two formations meeting front to front, and none of the literature I read does.

On determinism, the notes are specific. Box2D's solver depends on contact order, and its author made it reproducible across platforms with floats by banning fast-math, fused multiply-add and library trig ([Box2D determinism](https://box2d.org/posts/2024/08/determinism/)). He also warns that object-pool free lists reorder bodies after a destroy and recreate. Weiss et al. resolve thousands of contacts per step with a **Jacobi** solver (six iterations, averaging factor 1.2), which reads one snapshot and is therefore independent of processing order ([C&G 2019](https://web.cs.ucla.edu/~dt/papers/cag19/cag19.pdf)). They also note that a deterministic solver can deadlock in an exact head-on case. In JavaScript, `+ − × ÷` and `Math.sqrt` are exact IEEE operations and there is no FMA contraction, but ECMAScript only *recommends* fdlibm for transcendentals ([esdiscuss](https://esdiscuss.org/topic/es6-accuracy-of-special-functions)). Firefox's `sin` and `cos` have differed around the sixth decimal across operating systems ([Mozilla bug 531915](https://bug635044.bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=531915)). The sim must not call `Math.sin`, `Math.cos` or `Math.atan2`. It should store headings as unit vectors or as table-indexed angles.

## A collision design: bins as bodies, state-gated overlap, combat moves ground

### Bodies and the cost at 16,000–32,000 men

At 400 men to a battalion, 16,000–32,000 men is roughly 40–80 battalions, or 240–480 company blocks at six companies each. With 4–8 lateral bins per block that is about 1,000–4,000 bins (my arithmetic, ignoring cavalry and artillery differences). Each bin is an oriented rectangle, and its block's collider is the union of its bins. That allows a two-stage test, which I recommend. The broad phase uses block rectangles inflated by a speculative margin, in a uniform grid with cells about one battalion wide, or a sort-and-sweep keyed on `(minX, id)` with a stable sort. The narrow phase runs bin-against-bin SAT only for block pairs whose inflated rectangles touch. Rectangles in 2D need four candidate axes, and the axis with the least overlap gives the contact normal and depth. Recoil's oriented-box SAT for stretched footprints is the shipped precedent ([GroundMoveType.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/beyond-all-reason/RecoilEngine/master/rts/Sim/MoveTypes/GroundMoveType.cpp)). 0 A.D.'s circle approximation of square clearances would badly misrepresent a line 150 m wide and three ranks deep. Six to eight bins against six to eight per touching pair, over a few hundred blocks, is negligible at 20 Hz. I have not benchmarked it in JavaScript.

A tick is 50 ms at 20 Hz. A march at 1–2 m/s covers 0.05–0.1 m per tick, and cavalry at 5–8 m/s cover 0.25–0.4 m. Both are far less than a three-rank block's depth of about 2 m. Tunnelling is therefore not a real risk as long as each contact starts being considered once the gap falls below `relativeSpeed·dt` plus a small margin, which is Box2D's speculative-contact idea.

### Who may overlap whom: a state table, not a mass ratio

Mass-based collision fails in both directions, so I recommend that overlap permission depend only on the pair's state. Each body is **formed** or **loose**, and each is **friend** or **enemy** of the other.

| Pair | Rule | Precedent |
|---|---|---|
| Anything vs terrain or statics (walls, buildings, unlimbered guns) | Statics always win; a bin's motion is clamped at the obstacle | Recoil "only apply forces from static objects" |
| Blocks of the same battalion | Exempt from each other; the battalion layout owns their spacing | 0 A.D. control groups |
| Formed friend vs formed friend | Hard non-overlap. A moving body yields to a halted or engaged one and re-plans around it locally; when both are moving, they split the gap; exact ties are broken by lower ID | 0 A.D. moving/idle split and ID-parity tie-break |
| Formed enemy vs formed enemy | Hard non-overlap at the contact line. Contact opens an engagement, and only combat results move ground | No sourced precedent; design |
| Loose friend vs formed friend | Pass through. The overlap adds disorder to the formed body in proportion to the men overlapping, and slows the loose body | TW: Arena overlap cost; 0 A.D. `ignore` flag and pressure slowdown; Hagelberg rout "carrying the other battalions back" |
| Loose vs loose (friend) | Pass through, with pressure slowdown only | 0 A.D. pressure |
| Loose vs formed enemy | The loose body is blocked. Skirmishers retire, and routers caught against an enemy are captured or cut down | Design |
| Formed vs loose enemy | The formed body may enter the ground the loose enemy occupies (pursuit, overrunning a broken unit) | Design |

The passage of lines, routers, skirmishers and fleeing gunners all reduce to one rule: they are loose, and loose friends pass through. A passage of lines is an order that sets the passing battalion to loose for the duration. The battalion pays for it in disorder on both sides and re-forms on the far side. Grand Tactician is the shipped game praised for showing this ([GT Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/654890/discussions/0/787664008885842907)). Gunners who abandon their pieces become a small loose body, while the guns stay behind as static obstacles. A breakthrough is a state change in the target. A cavalry charge that wins does not push through a formed battalion. It breaks the battalion, which becomes loose, and only then may the cavalry ride over its ground. That keeps the invariant "two formed bodies never overlap" exact, and it removes Total War's pass-through complaint by construction.

### Resolution: clamp advances with min, then apply

Use clamps, not pushes. Each tick, every bin computes its intended displacement from its anchor and its lag state. For each contact between formed bodies, compute from the start-of-tick snapshot how far each side may advance along the contact normal. A halted or engaged side takes no share of the gap. The moving side takes all of it. Two moving bodies split it, either evenly or by speed. Each bin's allowed advance is then the *minimum* over all its contacts. IEEE `min` is exactly commutative and associative for non-NaN values (signed zero aside, which never matters here). So unlike summed forces, this step gives bit-identical results in any iteration order, without even sorting the pair list. That is my inference from floating-point semantics, not from a source. Pairs should still be generated in canonical `(minId, maxId)` order for anything that is summed: disorder from overlap, pressure, and events. Within a block, the bins then propagate their clamps sideways through the shear limit, in fixed bin-index order. Each block reads only its own bins, so this step is also independent of the order in which blocks are processed.

A chain such as a column pressing into a halted column needs more than one pass. Either run two to four Jacobi rounds on the snapshot, or accept one tick of latency per link and rely on the speculative margin. Rotation (a wheel) sweeps corners, so test the rotated tentative pose rather than only translations. Any residual overlap from rotation can be removed by a small Jacobi position correction with averaging. Weiss et al. show that this form is order-independent ([C&G 2019](https://web.cs.ucla.edu/~dt/papers/cag19/cag19.pdf)). Between formed bodies, use no impulses or knockback. AoE2 DE's experience with "Collide & Correct" suggests such corrections scatter formations.

### Bending and its limits

The bins are what make a line bend. A bin stopped by a contact accumulates lag behind the block anchor, and its neighbours keep advancing up to a maximum lag difference between adjacent bins. A line that meets an enemy column at one point therefore bows around it. A line that meets an enemy line along its full width halts evenly. This is the existing terrain-lag mechanism with "blocked by another body" added as a new source of obstruction. Following Henry et al., when the lag spread across a block exceeds a threshold, the anchor slows or halts so the shape waits for its bins. Past a second threshold, the block takes disorder instead of deforming further. I recommend letting outer bins of a halted block slide along the contact tangent so the flanks curl around an enemy's corner. That is a gameplay choice the literature does not test. Following Gu and Deng, bins should stop chasing their exact slot inside a small threshold, which prevents oscillation against a contact.

### What happens when two battalions clash

Most clashes in this period should be decided before contact. The earlier research supports a halt and a morale test at roughly 30 paces, with one side usually giving way. Only when neither side yields do the bodies meet. At that point the touching bins halt at the shared contact line, and the engagement records the contact length per bin pair. The number of men fighting is that frontage times men per metre, so only the front ranks along the touching segment count. This follows the Total War distinction between a collision radius and a larger combat-reaction radius, where engagement is measured separately from blocking. Each tick the combat model resolves casualties and a pressure result. When one side loses ground, its contact bins step back by a combat-derived distance. The winner's bins follow up, still clamped by the non-overlap rule, so the contact line moves while the bodies stay apart. A losing battalion with friends close behind it compresses against them and takes disorder, which is how "pressed back on the second line" emerges without any shoving. If it breaks, it becomes loose, and the routers flow through their friends at a disorder cost to those friends. That makes the historical rout contagion visible.

### Jams between friends

Friendly formed bodies can still deadlock, for example two columns meeting head-on in a defile. I recommend escalating in the way the engines do. First wait a few ticks. Then sidestep perpendicular to the contact normal, with the lower ID yielding, which is the 0 A.D. crossing nudge with a deterministic tie-break. Then re-plan locally, treating moving friends as transparent and halted ones as obstacles (OpenRA's ignore levels). Finally report failure to the order system, which suspends the move and flags it to the player. Never re-plan the long path on every contact, because that is the cascade that ground SupCom 1 to a halt. Do not give halted bodies an absolute "immovable" status with no exit. Recoil's push-resistant ships show that two such bodies lock each other permanently.

### Determinism checklist

The sim needs restricted float64 throughout, with no transcendental functions. Headings should be unit vectors or table angles. Pair lists should be built in canonical order with a stable sort, and every summation should run in a fixed order. Entity IDs must be stable and never reused out of order, which is Box2D's free-list pitfall. Any tie-break must come from IDs or a seeded shared PRNG. A per-tick state hash should be checked in CI on more than one browser engine. Quantising positions at the end of each tick, for example to 1/256 m, is optional, but it bounds drift and makes hashing trivial.

## Commands: plain replaces, Shift appends, and interruptions suspend rather than clear

### Three layers: orders, stances, reactions

Every shipped game sourced here separates queueable orders from persistent modes. BAR puts it most clearly. Movement state (Hold, Maneuver, Roam) and fire state (Fire at Will, Hold Fire, Return Fire) sit beside the queue. Wait "pauses a unit without removing its orders" ([BAR commands](https://www.beyondallreason.info/commands-20)). Total War's fire-at-will, skirmish and melee modes are toggles, not orders ([Rome II manual](https://r2enc.totalwar.com/en/manual/single-player/0092_enc_page_battle_play_interface)). In Grand Tactician, a line changes automatically to march column on long moves and back to line near the enemy, and its square "cannot move" ([magicgameworld GT guide](https://www.magicgameworld.com/?p=127343)). That is a shipped precedent for formation changes the system inserts and for square as a state that suspends movement. Move also comes in two kinds everywhere. StarCraft II's Move goes "without engaging enemies along the way", while Attack Move goes "fighting nearby enemies along the way" ([Blizzard guide](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/6640645/game-guide-simplified-controls)).

For this game, I recommend the layers in the table below.

| Layer | Contents | Queued? |
|---|---|---|
| Orders | Move (waypoints; frontage and facing set by drag at each queued destination; an engage-on-contact flag that chooses between halting to return fire and marching through), change formation, wheel, face about, dress, charge, assault, passage of lines, halt | Yes |
| Stances | Fire mode, auto-square against cavalry on or off, hold ground or pursue, permission to retire when wavering | No; persistent toggles |
| Reactions | Form square, fall back when wavering, halt at contact, rout, rally, skirmishers retiring | No; the sim inserts them |

Drag should set the end state (position, frontage, facing), and a move mode should set the transit. Total War couples the two, and players dislike the result. A Warhammer III player complains that units "spread out at their own speeds" and calls Alt-drag "pretty darn awkward" ([Steam WH3](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1142710/discussions/0/3827536762642191478)). BAR offers line-drag and move-at-slowest-speed as separate modifiers. A multi-battalion move should keep relative positions at the slowest battalion's speed by default.

### Queue semantics

The convention is the same in every source. SC2's Shift gives "a series of commands for the unit to perform in succession" ([Blizzard](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/4552955/game-guide-special-control)). SupCom's Shift+right-click "adds the current command to the end of the unit's queue" ([ludo.guide](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/supreme-commander/1-2-giving-orders)). Stop and Halt cancel everything. Two refinements are worth taking. The first is a front-insert. Spring's `CMD.INSERT` takes a position, where 0 is the front and negative positions count from the back ([Spring wiki](https://springrts.com/wiki/Lua_CMDs)). BAR binds front insertion to Space, and the unit "then returns to its original queue" ([BAR](https://www.beyondallreason.info/commands/queues-add-in-front)). Players miss this in SupCom's engineer queues ([Steam FA](https://steamcommunity.com/app/9420/discussions/0/3819662254699361587)). The second is a cap on queue length with visible feedback, which SC2 has. Keep the semantics identical in every context. CoH3 confused players because orders issued during Tactical Pause appended while real-time orders overwrote ([Prima](https://primagames.com/gaming/company-of-heroes-3-tactical-pause-guide); [Steam CoH3](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1677280/discussions/0/3770113150039372659)). Formation changes and facing should be able to attach to a queued waypoint. No shipped game documents manual mid-move formation queuing, so this is design.

Queue edge cases need tests. Age of Empires IV shipped with three Shift bugs: attack-move needed several presses of A, units ignored Shift points that were close together, and Shift did not reset after release ([Patch 15965](https://www.ageofempires.com/news/age-of-empires-iv-patch-15965/); [Patch 20249](https://www.ageofempires.com/news/age-of-empires-iv-patch-20249/)). Those are the cases to cover.

### What interrupts a chain, and whether it resumes

None of the sourced games clears a queue on combat contact. Clearing always comes from an explicit verb (Stop, Halt), from a retreat or withdraw order, or from a new plain order. Engaging during a move is either part of the order (attack-move, fight, patrol, where the unit fights and continues along its path) or a stance that overrides it ([BAR](https://www.beyondallreason.info/commands-20); [Rome II manual](https://r2enc.totalwar.com/en/manual/single-player/0092_enc_page_battle_play_interface)). The project's current rule, that a charge or melee clears the queue, has no precedent and throws away the player's plan on the most common event in a battle. No source documents any game resuming a queue after a forced interruption, so the classes below are my design. They follow the three-layer logic and BAR's Wait.

| Interruption | Examples | Queue effect | On end |
|---|---|---|---|
| Part of the order | Move with engage-on-contact halts to fire; a queued charge goes into melee | None; the order owns the fight | The order completes and pops, and the next entry runs if the unit still holds. A charge that wins and halts proceeds; one that ends in rout clears |
| Forced reaction under command | Auto-square against cavalry, halt at an enemy contact that blocks the path, melee the unit did not order, a friendly jam reported as failed | **Suspend**: keep the queue, show it dimmed | Resume once the reaction ends. Re-check the head order first: re-plan the path from the current pose, drop waypoints already passed, and restart a timed drill such as a wheel from the start. If suspension lasts past a tunable timeout, drop the queue and notify the player |
| Loss of command | Rout, broken, fall back when wavering (unless the stance permits it as a controlled retire) | **Clear** | After a rally the unit holds with an empty queue. The plan is stale and the player must re-issue it, as with CoH Retreat or Total War Withdraw |
| Player verb | Plain order, Stop, Halt | Replace or clear | — |

Two rules keep this predictable. A suspended queue should always be visible, so the player knows the unit will resume. A new plain order during a suspension replaces the queue as it always does. The display should follow shipped practice: colour-coded lines per order type (SupCom draws move blue and attack red, BAR draws move green, fight purple and attack red), plus, for a formation game, a ghost outline at each queued destination showing frontage and facing. Age of Empires IV shipped without waypoint markers and players asked for them ([GamingTrend roadmap](https://gamingtrend.com/news/age-of-empires-iv-road-map-released-details-plans-through-spring-2022/)). The two cheapest useful edits are truncate-from-here, as in CoH3's command bar, and delete-one, as with SupCom's Ctrl+Shift+right-click.

## Conclusion

The findings separate two questions that mass-based games merge: who may share ground, and who loses ground. The first is a fixed function of state (formed or loose, friend or enemy). The second is an output of combat and morale. Keeping them apart is what makes "fight, bend, never overlap" achievable. It also makes the collision step simple enough to be exactly order-independent, because a min-clamp over a snapshot does not depend on order the way summed forces do. The bin-lag model the project already uses for terrain becomes the deformation mechanism at no extra cost, and it is the one part with real support in the literature: group-level collision plus member lag. What nobody has tested is the clash itself, with two deformable formations meeting front to front. The numbers worth fixing early in a prototype are the shear limit between adjacent bins, the lag-spread threshold at which the anchor waits, and the disorder charged for each loose man passing through a formed friend.

On commands, the evidence is strong for the convention and absent on the question that matters most. Replace, append, clear, front-insert and a visible queue are settled practice. Resume-after-interruption is undocumented everywhere, so the suspend/clear split above should be treated as a hypothesis to playtest. The test that decides it is whether players re-issue orders after a melee more often than they cancel resumed ones. Whatever the answer, each tick should record whether the queue was suspended or cleared, so replays can show which rule fired.
