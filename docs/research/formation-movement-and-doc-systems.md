# Battalions flow when soldiers chase moving slots

A battalion stops looking like a rigid block when each soldier steers toward a slot that is itself moving. Three rules make that work. The slot is projected ahead of a formation anchor, so the soldier never quite reaches it. The soldier keeps the same file and rank through every manoeuvre, so neighbours stay neighbours. And the soldier has a bounded leash, so trees, walls, casualties and contact push him off his slot before the formation pulls him back. Every documented shipped system uses some version of this. Company of Heroes adds predicted leader poses, along-track speed matching, obstacle-softened offsets found by a short A* search, and per-soldier drift. 0 A.D. adds a formation controller, greedy slot assignment, per-slot "sloppiness" and an automatic switch to column. Total War adds per-man collision mass, and players watch its lines "blob" once melee starts. Historical drill fills the gap that games leave. Real battalions changed formation by having **company-sized sub-units wheel on guides, file off "en tiroir" or march obliquely, in a staggered sequence**. Regulation timings were about **30 s from column to square and about 100 s from line, stretching to 4–6 minutes in battle**. Most bayonet and cavalry "contacts" were settled when one side gave way before meeting. For this project that points to a two-layer design. The deterministic 20 Hz sim owns formation anchors, a small set of sub-unit "guide" anchors, shape, transition state, a disorder scalar and a per-battalion slot-occupancy table. A non-deterministic per-soldier layer at render rate does slot steering, separation, obstacle avoidance, terrain snapping, gait and the visible chaos of melee and rout, and writes straight into Babylon thin-instance buffers. On the second question, mature practice has dropped the monolithic design document. It uses a short vision-and-pillars page, one short living doc per system, append-only decision records, plans split into active and done, a research folder that is clearly not authoritative, and an agent instruction file under about 200 lines that works as a map rather than an encyclopedia. Section 5 gives a concrete layout and templates for that.

**How to read the tags.** [primary] means developer-authored material: articles, source code, patch notes, official docs or regulation text. [secondary] means press, wiki, fan or historian summaries. [forum] means player or modder reports. [unverified] means the research notes recalled the claim from background knowledge and found no source this session. [inference] means a design judgement drawn from the sources, not a sourced fact. Historical figures are drill-ground values unless marked as battlefield values. Most of the historical evidence comes second-hand, through footnoted compilations (Napoleonistyka, balagan.info, a wargamer's summary of the 1792 British regulations, an austerlitz.org reading of the French 1791 Règlement). The regulation texts themselves could not be fetched.

## 1. Shipped games buy fluidity with drift, softened slots and a melee blob

Company of Heroes is the one shipped formation system with a full public recipe, and every technique in it carries over to rank-and-file even though its squads are only about six men. Chris Jurney's chapter in *AI Game Programming Wisdom 4* lays it out ([Jurney, CoH Squad Formations Explained](https://forum.arongranberg.com/uploads/short-url/rWod3K2KhNWcOEdjewnsLXQKU6A.pdf)) [primary]. The leader alone paths to the click. Each follower's goal is its offset from the leader's **predicted position about two seconds ahead, rotated by the leader's future heading**. A speed modifier corrects error only along the direction of motion, slowing a follower who is ahead and speeding one who is behind; lateral error is ignored because "the follower's goal will bring him back in line eventually." Offsets are never used raw. A cheap A* search capped at **50–100 nodes** runs from the leader's future position toward the ideal offset, and the follower takes the nearest point the search touched. This makes the formation "squeeze in organically at chokepoints and route around small obstacles." Each soldier carries a **personal drift variable** that wanders within a maximum radius every tick, so the shape never looks machined. When a soldier dies or reinforcements arrive, roles are reassigned with **"the minimum number of swaps necessary"**, because it "looks awkward when a soldier randomly runs from the left side of a squad to the right." Repathing is triggered only when goal or speed changes beyond a delta, which works out to every **0.5–0.75 s**. Shape is chosen from the terrain under the leader: wide wedge in the open, tight wedge in confined ground, staggered column on roads. Near the goal a separate destination formation has each soldier *reserve* his spot, then search near it for cover against enemies in the facing direction.

Total War, which matters more for scale, has published almost nothing about how its formations work. What is known comes from modders and players. Since Rome, each soldier is an entity with collision and mass. The unit file sets close and loose spacing side-to-side and front-to-back, plus rank count ([Heavengames EDU guide](https://rtw.heavengames.com/rtw/mods/tutorials/export_descr_unit/)) [forum/modding docs]. Per-man mass decides who pushes whom, and high mass units "break through enemy lines easier and also hold against enemy pushing better" ([TWC EDU guide](https://www.twcenter.net/threads/the-complete-edu-guide.111344/)) [forum]. From Empire onward a `battle_entities` table gives each entity a collision radius; raising infantry radius by much more than about 0.1 makes pike formations "buggy", and men "jitter" while running because each keeps his own personal space ([Steam Workshop spacing mod](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=320217945)) [forum]. Melee uses per-pair matched kill animations, confirmed by a Rome II patch note fixing routers who turned to "play matched combat animations as they are being killed" ([Rome II Patch 9](https://wiki.totalwar.com/w/Total_War_ROME_II:_Patch_9)) [primary]. Warhammer III added "collision attacks" when entities collide while charging ([CA Patch 5.1.0](https://community.creative-assembly.com/total-war/total-war-warhammer/blogs/23-total-war-warhammer-iii-patch-5-1-0)) [primary]. The visible cost is well documented. In Shogun 2, melee "devolves into a blob and any formation you might have had is gone", and men at the back of the blob "aren't doing any fighting" ([Steam, Shogun 2](https://steamcommunity.com/app/34330/discussions/0/152391995401922447/)) [forum]. In Rome II, hoplites hold formation until about the last 50 ft and then break ranks ([Steam, Rome II](https://steamcommunity.com/app/214950/discussions/0/364040961450147235)) [forum]. Units holding formation while taking casualties "shift constantly to try to maintain the formation", so some players turn formations off before a fight ([Steam mod comments](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/comments/196851285)) [forum]. That last complaint is the strongest warning for this project: if closing ranks reshuffles the whole slot table, every casualty sets off a visible ripple. How Total War wheels lines, deforms them over slopes and woods, or refills slots has no public source. The recollection that units re-form by each man pathing to his new slot, rather than by a true pivot wheel, is [unverified].

The other titles each show one design choice and the artefact that comes with it. Bannerlord exposes its slot model in the official API: `IFormationArrangement` indexes slots by (file, rank), tracks "positioned" against unpositioned units, and adding a unit can fail when "deepening the formation does not create any new vacant positions" ([Bannerlord API docs](https://apidoc.bannerlord.com/v/1.2.7/interface_tale_worlds_1_1_mount_and_blade_1_1_i_formation_arrangement.html)) [primary]. Its AI has three layers (tactic, formation, agent), and the formation AI uses "the same interface and set of commands as the player" ([GameBanshee dev-blog repost](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k4h4h)) [primary repost]. Players say its shield wall mostly means "standing closer together and walking more slowly", and that formations dissolve once fighting starts ([Steam, Bannerlord](https://steamcommunity.com/app/261550/discussions/0/3493131640451139234)) [forum]. Cossacks and American Conquest make the formation a gated game object. An officer and a drummer (plus a flag bearer in AC) form fixed sizes of 15, 36 or 72 same-type soldiers, troops outside formation "blob together into tightly packed circles", and a formation whose morale hits zero routs and cannot be controlled ([AC fandom wiki](https://americanconquest.fandom.com/wiki/Formations); [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/AmericanConquest); [Worthplaying, Cossacks II](https://www.worthplaying.com/article/2005/7/30/reviews/26220-pc-review-cossacks-ii-napoleonic-wars/)) [secondary/forum]. The fixed grid scales to thousands of sprites and gives up fluidity to do it [inference]. Ultimate General draws a brigade as one deformable line ([DSOGaming](https://www.dsogaming.com/?p=52452)) [secondary]. Players report that routing brigades leave holes ([TWCenter, UG:G first impressions](https://www.twcenter.net/threads/first-impressions-ultimate-general-gettysburg.659538/)) [forum] and that units told to fire while flanking stop "in awkward positions" ([Steam, UG:CW](https://steamcommunity.com/app/502520/discussions/0/1479857071274157830)) [forum]. No Game-Labs source explains how the line bends over terrain. Holdfast, Scourge of War, Grand Tactician and Conqueror's Blade were not researched.

| Game | Core technique | Visible artefact | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company of Heroes | Leader path; goals at leader pose +2 s; along-track speed match; A*-softened offsets; drift; minimum-swap reassignment | Few reported; squad scale only | [primary] Jurney |
| Total War (Rome → WH3) | Per-entity collision radius and mass; data-driven spacing; matched combat pairs | Melee "blob"; ranks jitter while running; constant shifting under casualties | [forum] + [primary] patch notes |
| Bannerlord | (file, rank) slot grid; three-layer AI; each agent a full fighter | Formations dissolve in melee; weak shield wall | [primary] API, [forum] |
| Cossacks / AC | Officer+drummer gate; fixed 15/36/72 sizes | Unformed men clump into circles; rigid grids | [secondary/forum] |
| Ultimate General | Brigade as one deformable line | Holes from routers; awkward stops | [secondary/forum] |
| 0 A.D. | Controller entity; greedy closest slot; static sloppiness; auto column | Full re-layout on every death | [primary] source |

The pattern across these games is that the fluid look comes from soldiers being *late* to their slots, not from moving the slots more cleverly. The ugliest artefacts (blobs, constant shifting, men crossing the unit) come from assignment rules that ignore identity and from melee that releases every soldier at once [inference].

## 2. Real battalions moved as guided companies, slowly and raggedly

### A 100-figure battalion has a company's footprint

At one figure per soldier, the project's 100-man "battalion" occupies the ground of one historical company. **A French company of about 100 men stood 33 files by 3 ranks over about 18.5 m, at 22 in (56 cm) per file by regulation, spreading to 27–30 in (67–76 cm) in battle**. A 600-man French line was about 111 m nominal and up to about 139 m in battle ([balagan.info](https://balagan.info/battalion-line-column-and-square-tactical-formations-during-the-napoleonic-wars)) [secondary, citing Nafziger and Nosworthy]. Rank spacing varied by army: French 13 in (0.325 m), Russian 0.35 m, British one pace (0.63 m), Prussian 0.66 m, Austrian about 1.25 m. File closers stood about 1.3 m (2 paces) behind the rear rank (same source) [secondary]. Under the 1791 regulation a company that fell below 12 files formed two ranks instead of three (same source, via search snippet) [secondary]. For the game this means a battalion line about **18.5–25 m wide in three ranks, or 28–38 m in two** [inference]. Twenty to forty of them make a field of small, mobile blocks rather than 100 m walls. The drill sub-unit inside each battalion has to be smaller than a historical company. Four sections of about 25 men is the natural grain for wheeling, filing and staggering [inference]. A whole-unit wheel is still historically plausible at this size. The practical limit for a fixed-pivot wheel was **about 100 files** ([Rod's Wargaming](https://rodwargaming.wordpress.com/miltary-historical-research/military-historical-research/basic-formations-and-movement-drills/)) [secondary, quoting 1792 British regulations], and a 33–50-file battalion sits well under it.

### Every manoeuvre is wheel, file-off or oblique, run per sub-unit on a guide

Formation changes broke down into sub-units doing one of three things. They wheeled on a pivot man, faced to a flank and marched by files to a new spot ("en tiroir", like drawers sliding out), or marched obliquely. In each case a guide steered toward a marked point. In an open column the gap between sub-units equalled one sub-unit's frontage, so one simultaneous wheel turned the column into line. The 1792 British regulations say an open column "occupies the same extent of ground as when in line, minus the front of its leading division" ([Rod's Wargaming](https://rodwargaming.wordpress.com/miltary-historical-research/military-historical-research/basic-formations-and-movement-drills/)) [secondary, quoting regulations]. Under the same regulations every wheel from open column into line is made forward, and from line into column backward, so that sub-units of unequal size neither leave gaps nor crowd each other (same source). The Russian deployment, as described by Khatov, is the en-tiroir method: rear divisions face to one side, march by files until clear, face front and move up into line ([Napoleonistyka, Infantry Tactics 4](https://www.napolun.com/mirror/napoleonistyka.atspace.com/infantry_tactics_4.htm)) [secondary]. Nafziger says deploying a French attack column on its centre was the quickest method and deploying a column of pelotons on a flank was the slowest (same source). On "Forward!" French guides stepped out **6 paces** to set the alignment before the line moved off, and neighbouring battalions kept about **15.6 m** apart (same source).

Wheeling came in two kinds. On a **fixed pivot** the inner man marked time while inner files shortened step and outer files lengthened it. On a **floating pivot**, which the French used from 1791 and the British still lacked in 1804, the pivot man only shortened his pace, so the whole sub-unit kept moving. That needed NCOs at the corners and corporals at the quarter-company points as markers ([Rod's Wargaming](https://rodwargaming.wordpress.com/miltary-historical-research/military-historical-research/basic-formations-and-movement-drills/); [balagan.info](https://balagan.info/battalion-line-column-and-square-tactical-formations-during-the-napoleonic-wars)) [secondary]. A line could also wheel "in echelon", each company wheeling on its own so they arrived in turn from the pivot end. In the French 1791 "tournez à gauche/droite", the guide turned first and the men took up elbow contact again one by one ([austerlitz.org](https://www.austerlitz.org/en/?p=124)) [secondary, close reading with paragraph numbers]. A column of route followed the road: the head section followed the bends and each following section passed over the same ground (same source, École de Bataillon §162–163). When no landmark existed for a change of direction, the adjudant-major went **30–40 paces** out along the new line to serve as one (same source). Modern readers disagree about whether turns toward the guide were always 90° or could be any angle; the austerlitz.org author argues for any angle, citing roads that zigzag 20–80° (same source).

### Timings: seconds on the drill ground, minutes under fire

| Manoeuvre | Drill-ground time | Battlefield / note | Source tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attack column → square (French) | ≈ 30 s | — | [secondary] Napoleonistyka 4 |
| Line → square (French) | ≈ 100 s | — | [secondary] Napoleonistyka 4 |
| Hollow square, average battalion | 2–3 min | 4–6 min in battle; equal-faced squares up to 2× oblong | [secondary] Napoleonistyka 4 |
| Line → square (France 1808–15 / Prussia 1812) | 1 min 36 s / 1 min–1 min 24 s | disputed in thread | [forum, via snippet] TWCenter |
| Open column → solid square (British) | 42 s | disputed | [forum, via snippet] TWCenter |
| Cavalry closing to contact | ≈ 60 s | — | [secondary] balagan.info, carried from earlier research |
| Gun unlimbered and ready | < 1 min | limber up 1–3 min | [secondary, needs verification] |

For march rates, the earlier research found British ordinary and quick time at about **61 and 91 m/min** (30 in at 80 and 120 steps/min) and the French 1791 regulation at 76 and 100 steps/min ([Founders Online](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Hamilton/01-24-02-0343)) [primary letter, read via snippet]. This session's notes found no source for cadence and flag the widely quoted French charge step of about 120/min and British 108/min quick time as [unverified]. At 1.0–1.5 m/s an infantryman moves **5–7.5 cm per 50 ms tick**. A 90° whole-battalion wheel on a 25 m line moves the outer flank about 39 m, which takes roughly **26–40 s** at those speeds [inference, arithmetic]. In 20 Hz terms, a 30 s square is 600 ticks and a 100 s square 2,000 ticks, against about 1,200 ticks for an approaching cavalry charge [inference]. The gap between drill-ground and battle times suggests a **2–10× field multiplier**. If raw speed must stay readable, the multiplier can instead show up as a degraded intermediate shape, such as a partial square or a clump [inference].

### Guides, colours and file closers held the shape

Cohesion came from a few people placed on purpose. Each sub-unit had a guide on its directing flank who "conducts" it even at route step (École de Peloton §247) ([austerlitz.org](https://www.austerlitz.org/en/?p=124)) [secondary]. Markers went ahead to stake out the new line. In British drill the rear-rank man covered his file leader ([redcoat.org](https://www.redcoat.org/docs/exercise01.pdf)) [primary, via snippet]. File closers stood behind the rear rank to push men forward and fill gaps. The colour party at the centre as point of direction is standard knowledge but [unverified] in these notes. This structure is the historical equivalent of the anchor-and-slot architecture: a guide is an anchor that a sub-unit dresses on, and the colour is the battalion anchor [inference].

### Degradation, cavalry and rout

Lines degraded on any irregular ground. Chlapowski and Simmons describe ranks going ragged, battalions bowing and sometimes splitting in half, while columns got through broken or wooded ground; each street at Aspern was wide enough for only one platoon ([Napoleonistyka 4](https://www.napolun.com/mirror/napoleonistyka.atspace.com/infantry_tactics_4.htm)) [secondary]. Jomini wrote that French troops "have never been able to march steadily in deployed lines" (same source). Columns were not supposed to narrow below fours. When an obstacle forced it, they halted short, crossed in two files or single file, and re-formed on the far side ([Rod's Wargaming](https://rodwargaming.wordpress.com/miltary-historical-research/military-historical-research/basic-formations-and-movement-drills/)) [secondary]. Under sustained musketry "the infantry battalion would shrink toward its center and gaps appeared in the line", and men sheltering behind comrades turned a three-deep line into a disordered column ([Napoleonistyka 2](https://www.napolun.com/mirror/napoleonistyka.atspace.com/infantry_tactics_2.htm)) [secondary]. Smoke at Tellnitz meant "one could not see a single step" (same source). No source gave a time for re-forming after a hedge, ford or wall, and no memoir describes fording in formation.

Cavalry formed in two ranks at about 1 m frontage per trooper, with ranks about 0.67 m apart and squadrons a fixed **10 m** apart in French regulations ([Napoleonistyka, Cavalry Tactics](https://www.napolun.com/mirror/napoleonistyka.atspace.com/cavalry_tactics.html)) [secondary]. Approximate speeds were walk about 100 m/min, trot about 200 m/min, gallop 300+ m/min. In practice the gallop began **200–50 paces** out, and British regulations said to increase speed only while order held and to restrain the fastest horses so the line arrived together (same source). Heavy cavalry walked in close order and galloped only the last 50 m ([napoleon.org](https://www.napoleon.org/en/magazine/publications/napoleonic-heavy-cavalry-dragoon-tactics)) [secondary]. Galloping early opened gaps, squeezed horses out of the centre and broke long lines into groups and individual riders, because the best-mounted riders outran the rest ([Napoleonistyka cavalry](https://www.napolun.com/mirror/napoleonistyka.atspace.com/cavalry_tactics.html)) [secondary]. Contact was often never made. Du Picq (exaggerating) claimed one side fled first in about 49 of 50 charges. Costello describes two bodies riding through each other and wheeling to charge again. Jomini said victory went to "the side having the last squadrons in reserve" (same source). Infantry volleys at 20–60 paces turned cuirassiers back from squares ([Napoleonistyka 4](https://www.napolun.com/mirror/napoleonistyka.atspace.com/infantry_tactics_4.htm)) [secondary]. No regulation time for a rally was found.

Infantry melee was rarer still. Jomini saw bayonet melees only in defiles and villages, Larrey counted **119 musket wounds to 5 bayonet wounds**, and at La Rothière the columns halted at 30 paces until one broke when reinforcements came up ([Napoleonistyka 2](https://www.napolun.com/mirror/napoleonistyka.atspace.com/infantry_tactics_2.htm)) [secondary]. Routs spread from neighbours. At Hagelberg a Landwehr battalion gave way after about **3 minutes** of musketry, "carrying the other battalions back with them", and "panic gathered volume like a snowball" (same source). Fugitives threw away muskets ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/we-better-off-napoleon-never-lost-waterloo-180955298/)) [secondary]. Infantry caught by cavalry threw themselves flat or held muskets overhead, and at Borodino the 19th Jägers lay down while French cavalry passed, then got up and fired ([Napoleonistyka 4](https://www.napolun.com/mirror/napoleonistyka.atspace.com/infantry_tactics_4.htm)) [secondary]. At Kulm, Prinz August rallied several hundred fugitives by seizing a flag ([Napoleonistyka 2](https://www.napolun.com/mirror/napoleonistyka.atspace.com/infantry_tactics_2.htm)) [secondary]. That the rear ranks broke first is consistent with men sheltering behind the front but is not stated in any source found.

The historical record changes what "realistic" means for the game. Fluid does not mean free-flowing: real units were slow, staggered and ragged, and spent most combat time at musket range rather than in a scrum. A Total War-style melee blob is less authentic than a line that halts at 30 paces, wavers and breaks [inference].

## 3. Anchors, stable identities and leashed steering do the work

### A virtual anchor, not a leader, carries path, speed and turn limits

The standard pattern since Pottinger's 1999 Age of Empires article has three parts: a formation-level entity that pathfinds, a slot table of offsets in that entity's frame, and members steering to their slots ([Pottinger, Implementing Coordinated Movement](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/implementing-coordinated-movement)) [primary]. Pottinger's formation has three states, **Broken, Forming and Formed**, and cannot move until Formed. It fills slots inside-out, and a seated unit gets the highest priority so latecomers cannot dislodge it (same source). Mars and Chanut name the flaw in using a real soldier as leader: the leader "cannot reuse the exact same navigation process as an independent entity" because it must account for the group's bulk. They move the anchor to a **virtual group entity** whose maximum speed is below the slowest member's and whose maximum turn rate accounts for group width ([Mars & Chanut, Game AI Pro 2 ch. 20](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter20_Hierarchical_Architecture_for_Group_Navigation_Behaviors.pdf)) [primary]. 0 A.D. does the same in code: the formation controller takes its members' minimum walk speed and minimum acceleration and the largest member's passability clearance ([0 A.D. Formation.js](https://github.com/0ad/0ad/blob/master/binaries/data/mods/public/simulation/components/Formation.js)) [primary]. A 2026 RTS paper with this structure (one NavMesh path for the anchor, agents in its local frame) kept control logic inside 16.67 ms at **4,096 agents** in Unity Jobs and Burst. Rendering, not formation logic, became the bottleneck ([Athaillah & Kholil, Kinetik 2026](https://kinetik.umm.ac.id/index.php/kinetik/article/download/2643/124124512/124131330)) [primary].

The most useful single parameter in the literature is the **extrapolation horizon**. Rather than snapping members to slots, Mars and Chanut extrapolate each slot "using the group velocity over a time period greater than the frame duration", and "the extrapolation time period can be controlled to define the 'cohesion' of the formation, a small value for a tight formation a larger one for a very 'loose' formation" ([Mars & Chanut](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter20_Hierarchical_Architecture_for_Group_Navigation_Behaviors.pdf)) [primary]. Jurney's two-second prediction is the same idea. Their navigation pipeline runs slot-following, then a noise or personality stage, then collision avoidance, with each stage correcting the last. Members read group state from the previous tick only, which makes member updates independent and easy to multithread at the cost of one tick of lag per level (same source). For this project, the horizon can vary with context: short on parade, longer in woods or at the gallop [inference].

### Slot assignment: preserve identity for drill, optimise only for reforms

| Method | Cost at n=100 | Crossing / stability | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Greedy nearest slot | O(n²) | Late members cross paths or circle the group | 0 A.D. `TakeClosestOffset` [primary] |
| Sort by movement axis, then by row | O(n log n), effectively free | "Mostly" non-crossing, deterministic | Mars GDC 2014; Athaillah 2026 [primary] |
| Hungarian on squared distance | O(n³) ≈ 10⁶ ops, once per order | Collision-free straight lines given 2√2·R spacing (CAPT) | Xu 2014; CAPT [primary, abstract] |
| Identity-preserving drill map | O(n) | Neighbours stay neighbours by construction | No source; [inference] |

Greedy assignment "doesn't always work: the entities might have to cross each other's paths and the last entities might have to circle around the group" ([Mars & Chanut](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter20_Hierarchical_Architecture_for_Group_Navigation_Behaviors.pdf)) [primary]. The same chapter says global minimisation is O(n!), which is wrong. Linear assignment is O(n³) with the Hungarian algorithm. Mars's own heuristic sorts slots and members the same way and assigns them by index. Athaillah and Kholil refine that: rotate into the movement frame, sort front to back, split into rows and sort each row by x. They trade "global mathematical optimality for computational efficiency and deterministic predictability", which they say matters "for reproducible behavior in networked multiplayer" ([Athaillah & Kholil](https://kinetik.umm.ac.id/index.php/kinetik/article/download/2643/124124512/124131330)) [primary]. CAPT shows that minimising the **sum of squared distances** and moving every agent at constant speed on synchronised straight lines gives collision-free trajectories in open space, provided starts and goals are at least 2√2·R apart ([CMU RI: CAPT](https://ri.cmu.edu/publications/capt-concurrent-assignment-and-planning-of-trajectories-for-multiple-robots)) [primary, abstract only]. A linear-distance cost does not give this guarantee. Xu et al. add a term that penalises changes in the local Delaunay structure, so that neighbours stay neighbours ([Xu et al., CGF 2014](https://graphics.cs.uh.edu/wp-content/uploads/papers/2014/2014-CGF-CrowdFormationTransform.pdf)) [primary]. 0 A.D. sorts by class into contiguous blocks first. Its `fillFromTheSides` comparator returns a boolean, which `Array.prototype.sort` does not accept, so the resulting order depends on the engine ([0 A.D. Formation.js](https://github.com/0ad/0ad/blob/master/binaries/data/mods/public/simulation/components/Formation.js)) [primary, code observation]. Anyone porting that code into a deterministic JS sim should take that as a warning.

For drill, identity matters more than distance. If each soldier keeps his (sub-unit, file, rank) and the new slot comes from a drill rule ("sections wheel left in succession"), the transition reads as drill. A distance-optimal re-solve reads as a swarm [inference]. The Hungarian solve belongs only to "re-form anywhere" orders and to rallies, where historically men did run to the nearest place. At about 10⁶ inner operations it should take milliseconds in JS or WASM once per order, but no benchmark at n=100–200 was found [inference, unbenchmarked].

### Drill-like transitions need a scheduler, not a solver

No game or paper found models historical drill explicitly. The nearest pieces come from Bjore and from 0 A.D. Bjore builds a formation path from two **steering circles** whose radius equals the formation's turn radius, which guarantees "the formation will never need to make turns sharper than it is capable of" ([Bjore, Game AI Pro ch. 21](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro/GameAIPro_Chapter21_Techniques_for_Formation_Movement_Using_Steering_Circles.pdf)) [primary]. His "Column" rule (each man follows the man ahead in his file) is "fairly fluid… but it does not preserve the rows". His "Band" rule keeps rows: each row works out its turn direction, puts the inner-flank man behind his file leader and lays the rest of the row outward shoulder to shoulder (same source). The Band rule is a wheel on the inner flank, and the Column rule is a column of route following the road (§162–163). Bjore also treats formation points as pathfinding *targets*, not positions, so units can "go off and do other things… [and] resume pathfinding to its target position" (same source). 0 A.D. switches to column automatically past a walking-distance threshold. It re-forms on rotation only above `MaxTurningAngle`, staggers ranks with `ShiftRows`, and suppresses the centre move during the switch to avoid oscillation ([0 A.D. Formation.js](https://github.com/0ad/0ad/blob/master/binaries/data/mods/public/simulation/components/Formation.js)) [primary]. Academic transition methods (Takahashi's spectral interpolation, Kwon's group motion editing, Gu and Deng's sketched formations) preserve neighbourhood structure but target mass performances and expect keyframes ([Takahashi et al. 2009](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/CGF.v28i2pp639-648); [Xu et al. 2014](https://graphics.cs.uh.edu/wp-content/uploads/papers/2014/2014-CGF-CrowdFormationTransform.pdf)) [primary]. They are heavier than a grid needs.

Combined with the historical record, this gives a clear recipe [inference]. A formation change is a scripted timeline of sub-unit moves. Pick a pivot sub-unit. Give each other sub-unit a primitive (wheel on fixed or floating pivot, face and file-march, oblique march, or halt-and-dress on its guide) and a start delay that grows with its distance from the pivot. Each sub-unit's guide anchor follows a Bjore steering-circle path, and its men follow by the Band rule. Deploying on the centre then finishes symmetrically and faster, and deploying on a flank finishes visibly later at the far end, as Nafziger describes.

### Obstacles, terrain and rough ground: deform locally, keep the anchor rigid

Pottinger offers three obstacle strategies. Scaling offsets down fails for shoulder-to-shoulder lines. Ordered avoidance breaks the line and re-forms it. Halving the formation around an obstacle and merging beyond it "dramatically increases the visual appeal of formations" ([Pottinger](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/implementing-coordinated-movement)) [primary]. Mars and Chanut recommend group pathfinding that prefers paths the group can pass in its current shape but allows narrower passages, with the cost of narrowing comparable to the cost of a longer path. They also describe Karamouzas and Overmars's method of scoring interpolated candidate shapes (5 formations × 3 interpolants = 15 candidates) by an RVO-style evaluation ([Mars & Chanut](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter20_Hierarchical_Architecture_for_Group_Navigation_Behaviors.pdf)) [primary]. 0 A.D. merges split "twin formations" once their centres come close ([0 A.D. Formation.js](https://github.com/0ad/0ad/blob/master/binaries/data/mods/public/simulation/components/Formation.js)) [primary]. 0 A.D. also adds a fixed random `Sloppiness` jitter to each slot (same source). Henry et al. model the crowd as a deformable mesh, with mass-transport reassignment that weighs obstacles and slow terrain ([Henry et al., TVCG 2014](https://www.hubertshum.com/publications/tvcg2014formation/files/tvcg2014formation.pdf)) [primary]. No game postmortem describes the "line through trees: gaps, bunching, re-dress" behaviour. The design is therefore synthesis [inference]. The anchor and slot table stay rigid in the sim. Each soldier steers toward his extrapolated slot on a leash. Local avoidance against trunks and walls pushes soldiers off their slots, which produces gaps and bunching, and slot attraction re-dresses the ranks once the ground clears, with a speed-up allowance for laggards. A formation-level **disorder scalar**, a dynamic version of 0 A.D.'s sloppiness, rises in woods, villages and under fire, widens the leash and jitter, slows the anchor, and decays in the open.

Fords, bridges, village streets and gaps in hedges are clearance-limited path segments. The formation narrows to sections, fours or files there and re-deploys after, matching both 0 A.D.'s automatic column and the historical rule of halting short and crossing in files [inference, with [primary]/[secondary] precedents]. Slopes enter the sim as anchor speed and disorder modifiers. The renderer snaps each soldier's height to the heightfield and tilts him little or not at all [inference]. A line crossing a crest or a stream bank bows naturally once each soldier's speed depends on the ground under him in the cosmetic layer, which matches the historical "bowing" [inference].

### Avoidance: never run ORCA between men of the same formation

ORCA solves a small linear program per agent and claims thousands of agents "in only a few milliseconds" natively ([UNC ORCA](https://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ORCA/)) [primary]. A browser RVO2 WASM port runs about **3,200 agents** in a Web Worker and writes a WASM-heap `Float32Array` straight into a Babylon `thinInstanceBuffer`. The author gives no millisecond timings and reports heavy slowdown in OS power-saving mode ([Babylon forum](https://forum.babylonjs.com/t/improved-rvo2-wasm-port-performance-3200-agents-demo/62025)) [forum, author-reported]. The decisive point is qualitative: RVO enforces safety margins "that might forbid close formations", so avoidance must treat same-formation peers differently or consider "only imminent collisions" ([Mars & Chanut](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter20_Hierarchical_Architecture_for_Group_Navigation_Behaviors.pdf)) [primary]. Xu et al.'s heavier subgroup social-force model ran at 17 FPS for 1,936 agents ([Xu et al.](https://graphics.cs.uh.edu/wp-content/uploads/papers/2014/2014-CGF-CrowdFormationTransform.pdf)) [primary], so expensive per-agent physics does not scale to 4,000 without simplification. The split that follows [inference]: between formations, avoidance and contact use oriented boxes in the sim (20–40 bodies, trivially cheap). Within a formation, soldiers use slot springs plus short-range separation through a uniform spatial hash, which is O(n) with small constants.

### Casualties, melee and charges

0 A.D. handles a death by discarding its offsets and recomputing the whole layout from the survivors with greedy assignment. There is no rear-rank step-forward rule ([0 A.D. Formation.js](https://github.com/0ad/0ad/blob/master/binaries/data/mods/public/simulation/components/Formation.js)) [primary]. This is the mechanism behind the Total War complaint about constant shifting [inference]. The identity-preserving alternative follows the historical rule that the rear-rank man covers his file leader [inference]. When a soldier in rank r of file f falls, the man at (f, r+1) steps up and the file shortens from behind. When a file empties, files close inward toward the centre (the colours), matching "shrink toward its center". This costs O(depth) per casualty and moves at most two or three men. Jurney's "minimum number of swaps" rule is the sourced precedent for the principle ([Jurney](https://forum.arongranberg.com/uploads/short-url/rWod3K2KhNWcOEdjewnsLXQKU6A.pdf)) [primary]. For melee, Pottinger and Bjore both let members leave slots for subtasks and rejoin. The project's version would release only the front-rank leash along the contact line, pair those men with facing enemies as cosmetic duels, keep the rear ranks slotted and pressing forward, and restore the leash on disengage so slot attraction re-dresses the line [inference]. Combat itself resolves at formation level in the sim. History says contact should be short and rare, so the visual budget belongs in the approach, the halt at 30 paces, the waver and the break [inference from [secondary] historical sources].

## 4. Recommended architecture: the sim owns geometry intent, the renderer owns bodies

### What lives where

The sources support keeping authoritative state at formation level and individuals cosmetic, provided combat resolves per formation. Mars and Chanut frame group-level navigation as "top-down decision making" with per-member layers as corrections ([Mars & Chanut](http://www.gameaipro.com/GameAIPro2/GameAIPro2_Chapter20_Hierarchical_Architecture_for_Group_Navigation_Behaviors.pdf)) [primary]. Total War: Arena runs its battle simulation "at a lower rate than rendering" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/total-war-arena-maps-textures-optimization)) [secondary]. 0 A.D. is the counter-model: every member is a full simulated unit ([0 A.D. Formation.js](https://github.com/0ad/0ad/blob/master/binaries/data/mods/public/simulation/components/Formation.js)) [primary]. That is appropriate when individual positions decide hits. The earlier research for this project already settled aggregated per-formation musketry with render-only musket balls, Q16.16 fixed-point state in typed arrays, a worker-hosted 20 Hz sim and per-frame interpolation into thin instances (earlier report, sections 3–4). Under those decisions, per-soldier positions do not need to be deterministic [inference]. The one exception is the slot-occupancy table. At 100 bytes per battalion it is cheap enough to keep in the sim, and doing so makes "who fell" identical across replays and clients, which avoids a whole class of visual desync arguments [inference].

| Layer | Rate / numerics | State | Responsibilities |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sim: formation** | 20 Hz, Q16.16 fixed-point, seeded RNG, worker | Anchor pose and velocity; shape (line/column/square/skirmish/disordered); files × ranks; frontage per man; transition {type, start tick, sub-unit schedule}; disorder scalar; morale state; strength | Pathfinding with clearance cost; turn-rate limits; OBB contact and inter-formation avoidance; timed transitions with vulnerability; musketry, melee and charge resolution; terrain modifiers |
| **Sim: sub-unit guides** | 20 Hz, fixed-point | 4 guide anchors per battalion (pose and path progress) | Drill primitives (wheel, file-off, oblique, halt-dress) with staggered delays; split around obstacles and merge back |
| **Sim: slot table** | Event-driven | Per-battalion soldier→(sub-unit, file, rank), alive bitmask | Casualty selection by seeded RNG weighted to the exposed face; identity-preserving close-up |
| **Cosmetic: soldier** | Render rate, float, worker or WASM, non-authoritative | Position, velocity, gait phase, personal drift, leash state, animation state | Steer to slot extrapolated over horizon *h*; along-track speed matching; same-formation separation via spatial hash; static-obstacle avoidance; height snap; melee duel pairing; rout scattering; write thin-instance matrices and VAT frame |
| **Cosmetic: effects** | Render rate | Corpses, dropped muskets, smoke puffs | Purely visual, seeded per soldier ID for stable replays |

The interface between the layers is narrow: interpolated anchor and guide poses, the shape and slot table, the disorder scalar, the transition schedule, and event streams (casualty at slot X, contact begins, rout begins) [inference]. Because the cosmetic layer reads only previous-tick sim state, each soldier's update is independent. That fits a single worker loop over typed arrays now and a WebGPU compute pass later. No browser RTS has published such a compute-based formation layer [inference; no implementation found]. Keeping per-soldier history (lag, bunching) on the GPU needs state in a storage buffer, or ping-pong textures under WebGL2 [inference]. The working budget is roughly 4,000 soldiers × (one slot evaluation + about 8–16 hash-neighbour checks + one height lookup) per frame. That is well inside what the 4,096-agent Unity result and the 3,200-agent RVO2 port suggest, and both sources say rendering, not steering, is the limit. A local benchmark is still owed [inference].

### Recommended behaviours, in build order

| Priority | Behaviour | Sim side | Cosmetic side | Historical anchor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P0** | Anchor movement with extrapolated slots | Anchor path, speed ≤ slowest, turn-rate limit | Steer to slot at +*h*; along-track speed match; drift within radius | Guides 6 paces ahead set alignment |
| **P0** | Stable identity and rank closing | Slot table, promote (f, r+1), close files to centre | 2–3 men step up; no global reshuffle | Rear-rank man covers file leader; line shrinks to centre |
| **P0** | Wheel and change of direction | Steering-circle anchor path; Band rule; outer-flank speed cap | Inner men shorten step, outer lengthen (per-soldier speed scales with radius) | Fixed vs floating pivot; ≤ ~100 files |
| **P0** | Line ↔ column ↔ square as timed transitions | Sub-unit schedule with staggered starts; vulnerability flag; drill-ground time × field multiplier | Sub-units wheel or file-off visibly; flank deployment finishes later | Column→square ≈ 30 s; line→square ≈ 100 s; 4–6 min in battle |
| **P0** | Terrain conformance | Slope/ground speed and disorder modifiers | Height snap; per-soldier ground speed gives bowing | Ragged, bowing lines on broken ground |
| **P1** | Woods, villages and small obstacles | Disorder rises; clearance-cost path; may force column | Leash widens; avoidance opens gaps; re-dress on exit | Columns pass woods; lines split |
| **P1** | Defiles, fords and bridges | Auto-narrow to sections, fours or files; re-deploy beyond | Accordion: tail bunches, head re-dresses | Halt short, cross in files, re-form |
| **P1** | Musketry posture and attrition look | Formation-level fire resolution | Frontage widens over time under fire; men edge behind front rank as disorder rises | Spread 22 → 27–30 in; shelter behind comrades |
| **P1** | Infantry contact | Halt-at-range outcome first; short melee only on rare contact | Front-rank leash release, cosmetic duels; rear ranks hold | 119:5 musket-to-bayonet wounds; halts at 30 paces |
| **P1** | Cavalry charge | Walk → trot → gallop phases; impact plus morale shock; square multiplier | Spacing noise rises sharply at gallop; leaders pull ahead; ride-through or recoil | Gallop over last 50–200 paces; 10 m squadron intervals |
| **P1** | Rout and rally | Morale state machine; rout vector away from threat; contagion | Dispersing cone, dropped muskets, rally cluster around colours | Panic "like a snowball"; rally on a flag |
| **P2** | Skirmish screen | Open-order shape, pairs at about 15 paces (French) | Loose pairs using cover | Tirailleurs 200 paces ahead |
| **P2** | Leapfrog and personality | — | Occasional sub-unit or soldier variation | CoH leapfrog and drift |
| **P2** | Cavalry vs caught infantry, lying down | Event from charge resolution | Men drop flat; muskets overhead | Kincaid; Borodino 19th Jägers |
| **P2** | Countermarch, echelon wheel | Additional drill primitives | — | 1792 regulations |

The P0 set is what separates "rigid block" from "battalion". It is also the part that must be designed into the sim's data model from the start, because sub-unit guides and the slot table cannot be added later without changing serialised state [inference]. P1 behaviours are mostly cosmetic-layer work driven by sim events and the disorder scalar, so they can be added incrementally [inference].

## 5. Documentation works as a small map over many short, typed files

### The monolithic design document is dead; short, typed documents replaced it

Game designers abandoned the big GDD because nobody read it. Stone Librande's GDC 2010 talk argued that long design documents rarely get read past the first page, and that "wikis often break the core relationships between elements". He proposed one annotated diagram per system instead ([GDC Vault: One Page](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012356/One-Page); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-one-page-designs)) [primary/secondary]. James Sweatman of Jagex lists why central GDDs fail: they hold untested assumptions, every change to the game forces edits, developers read only their own section, and they make early commitments expensive. His alternatives are light user stories, early prototypes, live tools and research notes kept separate from decisions ([MCV/Develop](https://www.mcvuk.com/development-news/death-of-the-game-design-document/)) [secondary]. Design pillars, usually 3–5 player experiences rather than features, act as tie-breakers and filters against feature creep. Diablo III's postmortem is organised around them ([GDC Vault: Shout at the Devil](https://gdcvault.com/play/1017813/Shout-at-the-Devil-The); [Game Developer: design pillars](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/agile-game-development-part-2-design-pillars)) [primary/secondary]. How Relic, Creative Assembly, Paradox or Blizzard structure their internal docs is not public.

Engineering practice supplies three document shapes, and Diátaxis says not to mix them. **Design docs and RFCs** argue trade-offs before building. Google's version covers context and scope, goals and **non-goals**, design with a diagram, **alternatives considered** (which Ubl calls one of the most important sections) and cross-cutting concerns. It runs 10–20 pages for large projects or **1–3 pages as a "mini design doc"**, and Ubl says not to write one when prototyping is faster. Because "we humans are bad at updating documents", later changes go into linked amendments ([Ubl, Design Docs at Google](https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/)) [primary]. Rust's RFC template adds drawbacks, prior art, unresolved questions and "what if we do nothing" ([rust-lang/rfcs](https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs/blob/master/0000-template.md)) [primary]. **Decision records** are Nygard's five sections (title, context, decision in "We will…" form, status, consequences). They are numbered, never reuse a number, and are superseded rather than edited ([Nygard 2011](https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions)) [primary]. MADR standardises them under `docs/decisions/NNNN-title.md`, with status values proposed, accepted, rejected, deprecated and "superseded by" ([MADR](https://adr.github.io/madr/)) [primary]. **Reference and explanation docs** describe the current system, and their structure should mirror the thing they describe ([Diátaxis](https://diataxis.fr/start-here/)) [primary]. C4 gives four diagram levels (context, container, component, code) ([c4model.com](https://c4model.com/)) [primary]. The guidance that most teams need only the first two is widely repeated but [unverified] here. Docs-as-code keeps all of this in Git under review, and teams can block merges of features that lack docs ([Write the Docs](https://www.writethedocs.org/guide/docs-as-code/)) [primary].

### Agents need a short map and documents that load on demand

The AI-agent guidance agrees across vendors: keep the always-loaded file short and push detail into files that load only when relevant. Anthropic says to "target under 200 lines per CLAUDE.md file" because "longer files consume more context and reduce adherence", and that `@` imports do not reduce that cost because they load at launch. Subdirectory CLAUDE.md files and `.claude/rules/*.md` with `paths:` globs load only when matching files are touched. The test for each line is "Would removing this cause Claude to make mistakes?" Content that is inferable from code, frequently changing, or long-form tutorial belongs elsewhere. Emphasis should be rare, because "if you emphasize many lines, none of them stands out" ([Claude Code memory](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory); [best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)) [primary]. Contradictory instructions make the agent "pick one arbitrarily" ([Claude Code memory](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)) [primary]. Good task specs name the files and interfaces involved, state what is out of scope and end with an end-to-end verification step, and are best implemented in a fresh session ([best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)) [primary]. AGENTS.md is the cross-tool equivalent, with the nearest file winning ([agents.md](https://agents.md/)) [primary]. Claude Code reads it when no CLAUDE.md exists, and a CLAUDE.md can import it to share one file across tools ([Claude Code memory](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)) [primary]. Copilot supports path-scoped `*.instructions.md` with `applyTo:` globs, and its example generation prompt caps instructions at "2 pages" ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/how-tos/configure-custom-instructions/add-repository-instructions)) [primary]. llms.txt's curated link index with an "Optional" tier is designed for websites, but its shape fits a `docs/INDEX.md` ([llmstxt.org](https://llmstxt.org/)) [primary].

The most detailed agent-era case is OpenAI's "harness engineering" write-up, known here only through third-party summaries because the original returned HTTP 403 [secondary, may be paraphrased]. A single giant AGENTS.md failed: it crowded out the task and could not be checked mechanically. It was replaced by an AGENTS.md of about 100 lines acting as a table of contents, an ARCHITECTURE.md map, and a `docs/` "system of record" with `design-docs/`, `exec-plans/active/` and `exec-plans/completed/`, `product-specs/` and `references/`, with linters checking cross-links. The reported result was an internal beta in about five months with about 1,500 merged PRs and no manually written code ([codenote.net summary](https://codenote.net/en/posts/harness-engineering-ai-agent-era/); [daleseo.com summary](https://daleseo.com/harness-engineering/)) [secondary]. No empirical study measuring documentation drift, or comparing freshness mechanisms, was found. The structure below is therefore synthesis from practice [inference].

### The proposed layout for this project

```
AGENTS.md                    # ≤150 lines: build/test/run commands, ~10 hard rules, link to docs/INDEX.md
CLAUDE.md                    # one line: @AGENTS.md (plus Claude-only notes if ever needed)
.claude/rules/
  sim.md                     # paths: src/sim/**  → fixed-point only, no Math.sin/random, read docs/systems/<x>.md first
  render.md                  # paths: src/render/** → never write sim state; thin-instance conventions
docs/
  INDEX.md                   # llms.txt-shaped: one line per doc with status; "Optional" tier for research
  vision.md                  # pitch, 3–5 pillars, non-goals, art-direction link
  architecture.md            # C4 context + container; sim/render split; tick model; command pipeline
  conventions.md             # units (m, rad, ticks), coordinate frames, fixed-point format, naming
  glossary.md                # file, rank, frontage, guide, wheel, ploy/deploy, disorder, rout...
  systems/                   # one per system; CURRENT behaviour; 1–3 pages; kept true
    formations.md  formation-render.md  drill.md  terrain.md  pathfinding.md
    musketry.md  melee-charge.md  morale.md  command-delay.md  camera.md  ui.md ...
  decisions/                 # MADR-lite, append-only, NNNN-kebab-title.md
  proposals/                 # pre-build design docs/RFCs; frozen once implemented
  plans/active/  plans/done/ # per-milestone execution plans with checklists and verification
  research/                  # non-authoritative; reports and notes (e.g. this report); may go stale
src/sim/formation/AGENTS.md  # optional nested pointer: invariants for this module + link to systems/formations.md
```

The rules that keep this system working are few [inference]. Each fact has one home. Tunables such as frontage per man, drill timings and the cohesion horizon live in config files that the system doc links to rather than copies. System docs are the only documents that must be kept current, so they are kept thin and point at code and tests for detail. Proposals and decisions are frozen once accepted, and a changed decision gets a new superseding record, as Nygard and MADR prescribe. When a proposal ships, its durable content is folded into `systems/<x>.md`, the proposal is marked implemented, and the active plan moves to `plans/done/`. Research is explicitly non-authoritative, which matches Sweatman's split. The existing `research_notes/` and `reports/` folders in the blog workspace already fill that role and can be linked or copied into `docs/research/` with a date. Every doc carries front matter (`status`, `last_reviewed`, `decisions`, `code`). A small script or CI job checks links, flags system docs whose `code:` paths changed after `last_reviewed`, and fails on two instruction files that disagree. A recurring agent "doc gardening" task can run the same checks, as the OpenAI summaries describe. This fits the owner's existing plan → implement → review loop: the plan is a file in `plans/active/`, the implementation updates `systems/` and adds decision records, and review checks both.

### Templates

**System doc** (`docs/systems/<name>.md`, 1–3 pages; Google mini design doc + Rust RFC + Librande's diagram):

```markdown
---
status: current | draft | deprecated
last_reviewed: YYYY-MM-DD
pillars: [readable-battles, ...]
decisions: [0003-formation-authoritative-sim, 0005-identity-preserving-slots]
code: [src/sim/formation/, src/render/soldiers/, tests/formation/]
config: [data/formations.json]
---
# <System>
One-paragraph purpose and the pillar it serves. One diagram.
## Goals / Non-goals
## Constraints            (tick budget, determinism, browser/WebGL2 limits)
## Design                 (data model, update order, sim↔render interface, events emitted/consumed)
## Tunables               (link to config; never copy values)
## Alternatives considered (one line each; link decision records)
## Open questions
## Verification           (tests, replay hashes, debug overlays, how to see it working)
```

**Decision record** (`docs/decisions/NNNN-kebab-title.md`, MADR-lite, append-only):

```markdown
---
status: proposed | accepted | rejected | deprecated | superseded by NNNN
date: YYYY-MM-DD
---
# NNNN <Noun-phrase title>
## Context and problem
## Options considered
## Decision            ("We will …")
## Consequences        (good / bad / neutral)
## Confirmation        (test, metric or replay check that shows it holds)
```

**Plan** (`docs/plans/active/<milestone>.md`): goal; scope and out-of-scope; files and interfaces touched; a step checklist; an end-to-end verification step; and the system docs and decision records the plan will update. It moves to `plans/done/` when complete. **Proposal** (`docs/proposals/<name>.md`): the system-doc sections plus Drawbacks, Prior art and "What if we do nothing". Its status runs draft → accepted → implemented or abandoned. **Research note** (`docs/research/YYYY-MM-<topic>.md`): question, sources with tags, findings, and what was promoted into which decision. It is never cited as authority by system docs.

This report's recommendations translate directly into the first decision records [inference]: *0001 Formation state is authoritative; soldier positions are cosmetic*; *0002 Battalions have four sub-unit guide anchors in the sim*; *0003 Slot assignment preserves (sub-unit, file, rank) identity; Hungarian solve only for free re-forms*; *0004 Casualty close-up promotes from the rear and closes files to the centre*; *0005 Formation changes are timed sub-unit schedules with a field multiplier*; *0006 No ORCA between same-formation soldiers*. Each one is a separable choice that a later playtest might overturn, which is what decision records are for.

## Conclusion

The most useful finding is that "fluid" and "realistic" pull in the same direction once the right historical model is in place. The artefacts players hate in shipped games are melee blobs, constant re-shuffling under casualties and men crossing the unit. They come from treating soldiers as interchangeable and from staging long melees. Napoleonic drill avoided both: identities were fixed by file and rank, gaps closed from the rear and toward the colours, and most combats were decided at a distance. A design that keeps identity in a tiny deterministic slot table, schedules formation changes per sub-unit with historical timings, and spends cosmetic effort on approach, waver and break rather than scrum will look better and more authentic than a per-soldier physics simulation, at a fraction of the cost. The open risks are empirical rather than conceptual. No one has published wheel durations, re-forming times after obstacles, a rally duration, or a browser benchmark of 4,000 slot-steered soldiers, so the first prototype should be a single battalion that wheels, crosses a wood and takes casualties, with tunables in config and replay hashes in CI.

On documentation, the important point is that for a one-owner project the agent is the main reader. Every rule that protects human readers (short documents, one fact per home, status headers, superseding instead of editing) matters more for agents, because an agent never knows a document is stale unless the document says so. The proposed layout costs almost nothing to adopt now. Its value grows with each system added, because each new agent session can load one 2,000-token system doc and its decision records instead of reconstructing intent from code.
