# 0089 Battle maps sized by the armies' front: 2 to 4 km a side; Austerlitz 3 km (amends 0061 and 0070)

## Context

Decision 0061 sized the battle maps when a figure was a soldier: 3 km for the typical match of 4–5,000 soldiers, 6 km
for the extreme of 32,000, from the width of 600-man battalions (about 120 m each in line). Decision 0076 makes a
figure 25 men and a unit a division: a line unit of 240 figures in three ranks is about 50 m wide, where the real
division it stands for (about 6,000 men) held about 1.2 km in line. A corps of 8–10 units holds about 500 m of front.
0076 amends 0061's one figure a soldier but leaves its sizes, which were keyed to it.

The armies' fronts shrank by about 20 to 25 times; the maps did not. On the 6 km Austerlitz (decision 0070: a 2× model
of 12 km of field), the French army at 25 men a figure (73,000 men, about 2,900 figures) is about two corps, about
1 km of front where the real one held about 10 km: the field shrank by 2 and the armies by about 10, and the map looks
empty (the owner, 2026-10-30). Crossing 6 km at the sim's ordinary pace (0.9 m/s) takes almost two hours of real
time, against decision 0009's 30–45 minutes for a battle. The 32,000-figure extreme no longer sets a map size: it now
stands for 800,000 men.

## Decision

We will size a battle map by the armies' front, not by their figures: a side of **about twice one army's front in line,
at least 2 km**, so there is room to approach, to turn a flank and to hold a reserve.

| Side | For | One army's front |
|---|---|---|
| 2 km | one corps a side | about 0.5 km |
| **3 km** | **2–3 corps a side: the default** | about 1–1.5 km |
| 4 km | the largest battles, 4–5 corps a side (part of them in reserve or arriving) | about 2–2.5 km |

- This amends 0061's sizes (2 to 6 km becomes 2 to 4 km) and its examples; its other terms stand: square maps in whole
  kilometres, designed for play, soldiers, ranges and sight at real size, a game speed faster than real time.
- **Austerlitz** is a 3 km map: the same 12 km of field as a **4× model** (decision 0070 allows 1 to 4), heights scaled
  with it. This amends 0070's 2×. Its whole battle, about 6,300 figures, is the typical match's size.
- The owner chose 3 km for Austerlitz on 2026-10-30, from the armies' front, over keeping 6 km.

## Consequences

- Good: the armies fill the field; a flank can be turned in minutes, not hours; a battle fits 30–45 minutes at a
  moderate game speed (3 km at the ordinary pace takes about 56 minutes of real time).
- Good: smaller maps cost less to bake and draw; decision 0011's budget (1–1.5 km) is nearer, so the measurements
  0061 asked for are of a smaller step.
- Good: the figures on screen are Total War's (5–10,000 on 1–2 km), with the room to approach that 0061 wanted added.
- Bad: guns reach further across the field: roundshot to 1 km covers a third of a 3 km map (the owner's ranges,
  decision 0076). Guns are meant to hit hard; whether they dominate is measured in play.
- Bad: a 4× model halves the Austerlitz relief again: the Pratzen plateau, about 2 km across, is 500 m; folds that hid
  a man at 2× no longer do. Woods and villages stand out more against the hills.
- Bad: the 6 km Austerlitz (M8) is rebuilt at 3 km: its tools' inputs (ways, works, villages, ground, labels) are in
  map metres and are scaled and run again, and the owner's check of the map starts over.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep 2 to 6 km** (0061): the armies are lost on the larger maps; a 6 km map takes hours to cross.
- **Size by figures, re-keyed to 25 men a figure:** the figure counts stay those of a typical match, so every battle
  would get the same map; the front, not the count, is what the eye and the march measure.
- **Austerlitz at 4 km, a 3× model:** more room around the armies, at the cost of a front a third of the map's width;
  the fallback if 3 km is too tight.
- **Wider units** (looser files, or more figures a unit): the armies fill more ground, but the unit sizes are the
  owner's (0076) and loose files read as skirmishers.
