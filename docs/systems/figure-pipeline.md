# Figure pipeline: Tripo models to animated figures

How a model generated with Tripo becomes an animated figure: re-scale it, improve its rig, combine the
parts of a multi-part unit, author the clips in Blender by script, check every frame, publish a GLB and a
clip table, and record the figure in the registry. Everything is a Blender Python script run headless
(Blender 5.2.2), so every figure rebuilds from its untouched source. Built for three figures so far:

| Figure | Kind | Clips | Pipeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fr-line-infantry` | foot | 51 (march, fire drill, kneeling, bayonet, moves, falls, morale) | tools/unitpipe/infantry |
| `fr-foot-artillery` | crewed | a light gun and a crew of four: Idle, Load, Ready, Fire | tools/unitpipe/units |
| `fr-cuirassier` | mounted | the horse and rider: gaits, the sabre and the pistol (21) | tools/unitpipe/units |

Every figure and clip, with lengths, events and root speeds, is in `data/figures/index.json`.

## Layout (decision 0038)

| What | Where | In git |
|---|---|---|
| The pipeline's code | `tools/unitpipe/`: its paths, sources and registry scripts, `infantry/`, `units/` | yes |
| The Tripo sources (FBX and texture) | assets/tripo/<army>/<model>/ | no; listed by SHA-256 in `tools/unitpipe/sources.json` |
| Work files (Blender files, frames, logs, check results) | output/unitpipe/<pipeline>/ | no |
| Published figures (GLBs, clip table, videos, preview page) | output/figures/<figure>/ | no |
| The registry | `data/figures/index.json`, generated by `tools/unitpipe/registry.py` | yes |

`tools/unitpipe/paths.py` is the one place these are named (UNITPIPE_ASSETS and UNITPIPE_OUTPUT override
the two git-ignored roots). The sources come from the owner's Tripo exports: put each export's FBX and
its .fbm texture folder in its model folder, then `python3 tools/unitpipe/sources.py --check`.

## Commands

```sh
./tools/unitpipe/infantry/run-all.sh   # the infantryman: build, check, LODs, previews, publish (about 15 min)
./tools/unitpipe/units/run-all.sh      # the gun and crew, the cavalryman: prep, combine, animate, check, publish (2 min)
./tools/unitpipe/units/videos.sh       # their preview videos, published with the figures (about 30 min)
python3 tools/unitpipe/registry.py     # data/figures/index.json from the published clip tables (--check to compare)
python3 tools/unitpipe/sources.py --check
```

Each run-all stops at the first failing step (judged by its exit code, never by grepping its output) and
keeps every step's full log in the work folder. The infantryman's comparison with the versions that made its
accepted clips needs the archived Blender file of v8 in output/unitpipe/infantry-archive/v8/: without it it
fails, unless UNITPIPE_SKIP_COMPARE=1 says on purpose that it is not run.

## Constraints

- **Tripo output as given:** one skinned mesh, one texture, an auto-rig (Mixamo names for people, its own
  names for animals, a meaningless human skeleton for a gun). Equipment is fused into the body mesh.
- **Sources are never edited.** Clips a change does not touch must stay identical to the version that
  made them.
- **Game cost:** clips become vertex animation for instanced figures (`docs/proposals/engine-design.md` §7);
  props are extra bones on the same skin, never separate objects. A multi-part unit is one armature and
  one mesh.
- **Readable first** (`docs/art-bible.md`): clips read at the RTS camera; a prop too thin to read is made
  brighter or wider (the sabre's blade).

## The workflow for a new unit

### 1. Re-scale (units/prep.py)

Tripo normalises every model to 1 unit tall. Each is scaled to **metres** by one real measure taken on
the skeleton or a known part, never the bounding box (headgear and plumes would count):

| Model | Measure | Scale | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| gunner | hip joint at 0.90 m (as the infantryman) | 1.78 | 1.78 m with his shako |
| dragoon (rider) | hip joint at 0.90 m | 1.84 | 1.84 m with the crest |
| horse | back at the croup 1.56 m (a 15-hand cavalry horse) | 2.49 | 2.5 m nose to tail |
| light cannon | wheel diameter 1.35 m (a field carriage) | 4.08 | 4.1 m muzzle to trail, barrel 2.0 m |
| pistol (a prop, not rigged) | overall length 0.35 m (a cavalry pistol) | 0.35 | barrel turned to +Y, the grip's centre at the origin, decimated to 1,200 triangles |

The scale is applied to mesh and armature (no object scale reaches the export), and every model faces
**−Y** (the horse came facing −X); ground at z = 0. The infantryman, built first, keeps its model units
(1 unit = 1.82 m, in its clip table as m_per_unit); the registry converts its root speeds to m/s.

### 2. Improve the rig (units/prep.py)

Tripo's auto-rig is a starting point, never trusted as is:

- **Leaking weights** (`clean_weights`): an influence is dropped when its bone is far from the vertex
  (16 cm for people, 45 cm for the horse) and 2.5 times farther than the vertex's nearest bone. It found
  the gunner's thumb weighted to his right toe (the thumb stretched whenever the foot moved).
- **Coat skirts follow the arms** (`free_coat_from_arms`): vertices between 0.45 and 1.08 m that are not
  mainly arm lose their arm influences.
- **Props become rigid bones**: the gunner's sponge-rammer (its loose part found by shape), the rider's
  scabbard on a bone of its own at its mouth, hung from the hips, so a draw can swing it on its slings,
  and stretched from Tripo's 69 cm to 98 cm to hold the 97 cm blade; the sabre's hilt rides in it (hidden
  while the sabre is out).
- **The horse's tail** is re-skinned to the root and its two bones (it was half on the hind legs); bones
  renamed from `tripo::0_Left_Limb_2` and `bone_17` to `Horse:FL3`, `Horse:FR4`… (front or hind, left or
  right, joint 1–6).
- **A rig that is wrong is rebuilt**: the cannon's human skeleton is dropped and the gun re-rigged from its
  loose parts: `Gun`, `Barrel` (on the trunnions), `WheelL`, `WheelR` (on the axle).
- The **modelled grip** is recorded: where the gunner's right fist holds his staff becomes the grip every
  hand uses on a rod. For open hands (the rider's gloves) the grip comes from the hand's bones and is
  closed by a finger curl.

### 3. Combine (units/combine.py)

- **Battery:** the gun, four copies of the gunner (bones prefixed `Sponger:`, `Loader:`, `Ventsman:`,
  `Firer:`; only the sponger keeps the staff) at their posts (units/battery_layout.py), and two made props,
  the cartridge and the linstock. 48,100 triangles.
- **Cavalry:** the horse, the rider (`Rider:`), a sabre on its own bone, and tack the Tripo horse lacks: a
  shabraque and saddle cut from the horse's own skin and pushed out along its normals (so they fit and
  deform with it), a rolled cloak, and reins whose ends are skinned one to the horse's head and one to the
  rider's hand, so they stretch between the two; a **pistol** on its own bone and its **holster** at the
  front of the saddle on the right, where French cavalry carried it; the pistol's rest pose is in it, as its
  child, so it sits there in every clip that does not draw it. About 21,700 triangles (the pistol decimated
  from Tripo's 10,000 to 1,200 in the prep).
- Copies of one model share one material; UV layers get one name first. **Hiding a prop** is its bone
  scaled to 0.1%, parked at a fixed point so joins stay exact.

### 4. Animate (units/anim_battery.py, units/anim_cavalry.py)

Poses are solved per frame by script and keyed on every bone:

- **People** (units/biped.py): hips, chest and head as numbers; feet as ankle targets; hands as grips on a
  moving prop, a hanging arm, a wrist point, or any rod. Two-bone IK with elbow and knee poles.
- **Timelines** (units/timeline.py): key poses blended with an ease. **Footsteps** are separate: a foot
  stays planted until a step lifts it (it rises before it travels, so no scuffing). A walk of more than
  0.8 m turns into the path, walks facing where it goes and turns to its work in the last two steps; the
  hips follow the feet on a path of their own, so hand keys inside a walk keep their timing. A prop can be
  **carried** with a body. Hands are resolved at both keys and blended.
- **The horse** (units/horse.py): each leg is solved in its vertical plane from its fetlock target and the
  flexion of the carpus or hock. Gaits are phase tables at the ruleset's paces (`src/rulesets/napoleonic.ts`):
  in stance a hoof moves back at exactly the gait's speed, in the swing it leaves and lands at that speed,
  and the body is **lowered automatically** until every planted leg reaches its hoof (the front legs are
  nearly straight at rest).
- **Weapons in the rider's hand** (units/arm.py) are posed by the arm's joints, not by the weapon: an arm
  state gives the wrist's direction and distance from the shoulder in the chest's frame, the elbow's swivel
  (+ out to the arm's side), the forearm's turn (pronation, −85° to 90° from the handshake) and the wrist's
  bend (flexion within ±60°, deviation from −20° to 35°), and the weapon follows the fist (the sabre's blade
  continues the thumb's line, leaning towards the fingers by its roll in the hand; the pistol's barrel
  continues the fingers). Blending states moves the hand on arcs about the shoulder. A state can instead
  name the blade's direction: the swivel, turn, bend and (for the sabre) roll that point the weapon there,
  near the authored swivel and roll and with the wrist nearest neutral (steeply dearer past 36°), are
  searched once before any frame is posed (`aim_wrist`). Placing the hand by a point and a weapon direction,
  as before, bent the wrist 90 to 150 degrees.
- **A hand on a weapon that is still in its scabbard or holster** is placed by the weapon: a grip round a rod
  wraps it from the side that keeps the wrist straightest, remembering its last choice so the hand does not
  flip (`Biped.natural_grip`), and the elbow goes where the forearm best lines up with the hand
  (`Arm.easiest_elbow`). Where a draw hands over to arm states, the two methods differ a little; the
  difference (wrist, hand, elbow, the weapon in the hand) is applied in full there and fades out by the
  draw's end, so nothing jumps.
- **The sabre**: a cut needs an over-the-head state between the wind-up and the blow: two nearly opposite
  blade directions blended directly leave the swing's path undefined. In the draw the blade lies in the
  scabbard and slides out along it, the tip clearing it at `drawn`; the scabbard swings forward on its slings
  towards the drawing hand and slides down them as far as the arm is short for the blade (Tripo's arm is
  47.5 cm from shoulder to wrist; the grip must travel 1.09 m), then the arm carries the blade over to the
  guard while the scabbard falls back.
- **The pistol** is posed the same way, its lock's top asked to face up. In the draw the hand takes the butt
  in the aim's grip; the holster points at the shoulder, as a pommel holster does, so the rider sits back and
  turns his shoulder back as he pulls it out. Its clips in the shape of
  decision 0037: DrawPistol and HolsterPistol (from and to Idle), ReadyPistol (a loop, muzzle up),
  PresentPistol (the wind-up the game stretches), AimPistol (a loop it holds), LowerPistol (an aim called off,
  back to the ready), FirePistol (anchored on its `shot` at frame 3: the kick, back to the ready; the horse
  starts at the shot).
- Every clip that chains starts and ends on a pose its neighbours share; reactions (a horse's toss at a
  blow) are zero at both ends.

The clips of each figure, their chains and events: `data/figures/index.json`.

**Events** are 1-based frames of their clip (a clip's key k is its frame k + 1). Their meaning is the same for
every figure: `shot` (the charge fires: the flash, the recoil starts), `hit` (a blow lands), `parry` (a blow is
taken), `drawn` (a weapon clears its scabbard or holster), `sheathed` and `holstered` (from this frame it is
fully back in), `cartridge_out` and `cartridge_in` (the loader's cartridge appears, is rammed out of sight).
The game anchors clips on them (decision 0037).

### 5. Check (units/validate.py)

On the deformed mesh, every frame of every clip: nothing below the ground (−2 cm); a hoof or boot on the
ground moves less than 2.5 cm a frame over it, root motion included (a hoof's lift-off frame folds about
the fetlock, not the toe: up to 2.2 cm at the walk); loops close and every listed join matches (< 1 mm); the rider's right wrist (the angle between the forearm and
the hand's fingers) stays within 40° in every clip that holds or uses a weapon and 70° while drawing or
putting one back (`wrist_deg` in the check's output); the crew's wrists are reported, not yet checked.
Numbers do not replace looking: the stretched thumb, the flaring coat, the kicking tail, the fist below
the sabre's grip and the crew all facing one way were found in renders, not by a check.

### 6. Publish (units/publish.py, units/roundtrip.py, units/videos.sh, registry.py)

One GLB per unit (one skin, every clip as a glTF animation at 24 fps) and its clip table, in
output/figures/<figure>/; a reimport checks every clip is there. Then `tools/unitpipe/registry.py` writes the
registry, committed with the change.

Authoring aids (units/): inspect_units.py (bones and sizes of every source), components.py (loose
parts), views.py and closeup.py (renders), preview.py and grid.py (frames of a clip, tiled), joindiff.py
(which bones differ where one clip should join another).

## The infantryman (tools/unitpipe/infantry)

Built before the units' toolkit, in model units (1.82 m), with its own key-pose system; the authoring
guide is tools/unitpipe/infantry/AUTHORING.md.

- **Skin fixes** (build.py): the musket gets its own `Musket` bone and weight 1, the pack is rigid on
  `Spine2`, coat skirts that followed the arms move to `Hips`, finger weights move to the forearm.
- **Rig additions:** `GunControl` drives the musket; anchor bones mark points on it (`StockContact`,
  `TriggerGrip`, `ForeGrip`, `CarryGrip`, `PortGrip`, `BarrelMuzzle`, `BayonetTip`); a ramrod is a small
  made mesh on its own bone.
- **Key-pose authoring** (drill.py): a clip is a list of key poses (hips, chest, feet, head, the weapon as
  an anchor and a target, each hand on a weapon point, the ramrod or free), re-solved every frame, so
  hands stay locked to a moving weapon. Clip modules (kneel.py, moves.py, falls.py, morale.py) use the
  shared toolkit. Walks are speed-locked at the ruleset's ordinary pace; runs at the soldier layer's.
- **Turns are a whole-pose rotation** about the root; the clip table gives `root_turn`.
- **Preparing and shooting are separate clips** (Present, Aim as a loop, Fire), so the game sets how long
  a unit takes to aim.
- **Checks:** validate-drill.py checks every frame (hands on their points, joins, rigid weapon and
  ramrod, ground, wrists, pops, the left cuff clear of the stock while aiming, readability minimums);
  validate.py the older clips; roundtrip.py the GLB; compare-accepted.py that untouched clips are
  identical to the archived versions; lod.py and validate-lod.py three LODs (9.7k, 1,200 and 248
  triangles) on every clip, and every published LOD GLB reimports with every clip. DRILL_DEBUG=1 prints unreachable grips instead of failing.

## Verification of this layout

Rebuilt from the sources in the new layout, every clip table, check result and contact table is
byte-identical to the ones published from the old untracked folder, and the units' GLBs are
byte-identical (`docs/plans/active/figure-pipeline.md`). `test/figures-index.test.ts` checks the registry:
lengths, loops, events inside their clips, every chain naming a clip of its figure.
