Interface language

Ink on warm paper, as a staff officer would draw it, set in modern type. Every element of the game's interface and its states. New elements are added to the theme and to this sheet together.

00 Principles

  1. Paper and ink. Warm paper, warm graphite. Nothing on the interface is coloured except the armies, and one accent for what the player has chosen.
  2. The ticked frame. A hairline box with crosshair ticks at two corners, as on a surveyor's plate. The ticks grow when the element has attention.
  3. Pencil means not yet. What cannot be used yet is drawn in pencil: grey and dashed. It stays visible but cannot be chosen.
  4. Flat. No shadows, bevels, glows or rounded corners. Weight comes from line and fill.
  5. Modern type. Condensed grotesk for titles, a grotesk for text, mono for labels and numbers. No imitation of period type.
  6. Quiet motion. Ticks grow in 350 ms; screens rise 6 px as they appear. Reduced motion turns both off.

01 Colour

Neutrals do all the work. The accent appears only on selection and focus. Army colours come from data; nothing in the code names an army.

08 Bars and gauges

A unit's metrics are continuous bars, one colour each, from the period's washes: bistre for toughness (the men left of the battalion's), verdigris for morale, a carmine-and-indigo violet for fatigue. One plain style for every unit and value, and the numbers always written beside them: current / max.

12 Experience levels

The unit model's six levels (docs/proposals/unit-model.md): Recruits, Line, Veterans, then the Young, Middle and Old Guard. Ink glyphs that read as a scale at 16 px without colour: the line's levels are counted marks, the Guard's one family of a star, so the step into the Guard reads as a change of kind (as the period kept service chevrons and the Guard apart). Every mark is filled: a bar, one and two chevrons; then a star, a star over a bar, a star in a laurel.

13 Icons

Ink glyphs at 16, 24 and 36 px; hover one for its name. Six categories: line infantry (a shako), light infantry (the light infantry's hunting horn), heavy cavalry (a helmet with its mane), light cavalry (a horse), heavy artillery (the staff map's gun with its shot), light artillery (the gun under a horseshoe). Weapon mode: crossed sabres for melee, a musket for volley fire, a rifle for aimed fire. Fight mode: fire at will (the range ring), hold fire (crossed through). Shapes are drawn as Total War draws formations, each rank a line. Metrics head the unit bars.
