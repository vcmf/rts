Interface language

Ink on warm paper, as a staff officer would draw it, set in modern type. Every element of the game's interface and its states. New elements are added to the theme and to this sheet together.

00 Principles

  1. Paper and ink. Warm paper, warm graphite. Nothing on the interface is coloured except the armies, and one accent for what the player has chosen.
  2. The ticked frame. A hairline box with crosshair ticks at two corners, as on a surveyor's plate. The ticks grow when the element has attention.
  3. Pencil means not yet. What cannot be used yet is drawn in pencil: grey and dashed. It stays visible but cannot be chosen.
  4. Flat. No shadows, bevels, glows or rounded corners. Weight comes from line and fill.
  5. Modern type. Condensed grotesk for titles, a grotesk for text, mono for labels and numbers. No imitation of period type.
  6. Quiet motion. Ticks grow in 350 ms; screens rise 6 px as they appear. Reduced motion turns both off.

01 Colour

Neutrals do all the work. The accent appears only on selection and focus. Army colours come from data; nothing in the code names an army.

03 Lines and frames

Three weights: the hairline rule, the ink line, the bold underline of a chosen tab. The double rule closes a title. The frame is the container for everything that can be chosen.

05 Menu items

The home screen's choices: a number, a title, one line on what it holds. The chosen one is in the accent; muted ones are drawn in pencil and cannot be chosen by pointer, Tab or arrows.
